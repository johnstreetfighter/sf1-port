## US arcade flyers by Capcom USA: how many versions, and what each shows

### Takeaway
TAFA holds two US/English Capcom flyers. One is a single-game, two-sided flyer (A), printed in Japan with Capcom USA's Sunnyvale address, a painted cover and a back page that sells both the pneumatic "Deluxe Upright" and a button "Regular Upright". The other is Capcom USA's "Summer '87" three-game flyer (B), which shows Street Fighter only as a photograph of players at the pad cabinet plus screenshots. I found no separate dedicated "six-button kit" flyer: flyer A covers both control schemes.

### Cited Findings
- KLOV lists eight flyers for the game: one "EU" two-pager (1037), one USA "Sizzle of Summer '87" two-pager (4390), and six UK Electrocoin items (3977, 4007, 4008, 4009, 4019, 3959) — [KLOV game page](https://www.arcade-museum.com/Videogame/street-fighter)
- **Flyer A, front (my description of the scan):** a large painted illustration, apparently hand-painted in gouache or acrylic rather than smooth airbrush, with visible brushwork in the flames and drapery. A white-gi, black-belt fighter with wild brown hair and a white headband (Ryu, drawn in the realistic early style rather than the in-game sprite look) flies across the top in a jumping kick, wrapped in orange-yellow flames. Lower left, a ninja-like figure in brown wraps (Geki-like) somersaults. Centre, a shirtless brawler grapples a man in red. A Japanese pagoda/temple roof sits behind them, and torn-paper edges reveal the Mount Rushmore heads on the right. The flame-brush STREET FIGHTER logo runs at upper left with "TM © CAPCOM". The bottom third has a cutout photo of the curved deluxe cabinet, three in-game screenshots (Japan temple stage, US city "…CK HEADS" stage, a sunset stage with a ninja) and a yellow "Features" panel (simultaneous play, buy-in, continue). The footer gives addresses for **Capcom USA Inc., 1283-C Mountain View/Alviso Road, Sunnyvale CA** and Capcom Co., Ltd. Osaka, with "PRINTED IN JAPAN" and a blank "DISTRIBUTED BY:" box — [TAFA 1037 front scan](https://flyers.arcade-museum.com/videogame-flyers/1/street-fighter-01037-01.jpg)
- **Flyer A, back:** the headline is a condensed italic blue line telling players to strike the pads with a fist and noting they are pneumatically controlled. Sections are marked by purple rounded-pill labels: "Deluxe Upright" (cabinet photo; diagram of an 8-way joystick, a punch pad and a kick pad; a cartoon fist hitting a pad with a "Bang!" sound word) and "Regular Upright", in a red dashed box that describes buttons for punches and kicks. That box's diagram shows six buttons labelled PUNCH jab/hook/fierce and KICK block/round/fierce. The early names differ from the later Jab/Strong/Fierce and Short/Forward/Roundhouse. Blue tables give the joystick-plus-pad directions. A large red condensed slogan says Ryu and Ken challenge 10 street fighters from 5 countries. The lower half is a **flat cel-style cartoon character map**: Ken (red gi, blond) and Ryu (white gi, red hair) stand centre, and screenshot cards with red country tags (Japan, U.S.A., England, China, Thailand "?") link to cartoon busts of Retsu, Geki, Joe, Mike, Birdie, Eagle, Lee and Gen. Sagat and Adon are hidden behind a "?" card. A "Game rules" column sits beside it — [TAFA 1037 back scan](https://flyers.arcade-museum.com/videogame-flyers/1/street-fighter-01037-02.jpg)
- The flyer advises players to hit the pneumatic pads with a closed fist, with a "Bang!" sound word on the Deluxe cabinet (a secondary source confirming the scan) — [Wikipedia mirror, Street Fighter (video game)](https://ipfs.ssi.eecc.de/ipns/en.wikipedia-on-ipfs.org/wiki/Street_Fighter_(video_game))
- Wikipedia describes two cabinets: a "Regular" six-button version and a "Deluxe" with two pressure-sensitive rubber pads — [Wikipedia mirror](https://ipfs.ssi.eecc.de/ipns/en.wikipedia-on-ipfs.org/wiki/Street_Fighter_(video_game))
- **Flyer B, "The Sizzle of Summer '87" (Capcom USA):** a hot orange-red ground crossed by a yellow diagonal band. The headline is in a white casual brush script with a drop shadow ("The Sizzle of … Summer 87' is HOT with CAPCOM!"), with heavy black italic grotesque sans for the body slogans. The front shows the Bionic Commando and 1943 marquee art. On the back, Street Fighter has **no painted art**: the centre is a dark photo of three young men at the curved cabinet, with the blue/white "STREET FIGHTER" topper and round pads on the panel. Four tilted screenshots with black monitor bezels (temple, city, Mount Rushmore, tree/sunset stages) frame it. The caption calls "Streetfighter" (spelled as one word) a game that packs a lot of punch. The footer reads "CAPCOM Engineering… designs today for your profitable tomorrow", Capcom USA, 1283-C Mountain View/Alviso Rd., Sunnyvale, CA 94089 — [TAFA 4390 front](https://flyers.arcade-museum.com/videogame-flyers/4/street-fighter-1943-the-battle-of-midway-bionic-co-04385-01.jpg); [TAFA 4390 back](https://flyers.arcade-museum.com/videogame-flyers/4/street-fighter-1943-the-battle-of-midway-bionic-co-04385-02.jpg); [TAFA entry](https://flyers.arcade-museum.com/videogames/show/4390)
- TAFA's source credits: flyer A from Joseph Schnell; flyer B is a "TAFA Original" — [TAFA 1037](https://flyers.arcade-museum.com/videogames/show/1037); [TAFA 4390](https://flyers.arcade-museum.com/videogames/show/4390)

### Inferences
- Flyer A looks like Capcom Japan's English-language export flyer: printed in Japan, with both Capcom USA and Capcom Japan addresses and a blank distributor box for overseas dealers. That is probably why TAFA calls it "EU" while its address is American. It was likely the main US flyer too, but I did not confirm whether a US-only reprint exists.
- The painted cover uses a different visual language from the back page and the US cabinet art. The cover is realistic, painterly and Japanese-made, with flames and a gritty Ryu. The back page and cabinet art use flat cel-cartoon figures with clean black outlines and spot colour.
- The "Regular Upright" six-button panel already appears in the first flyer, so the button version was a launch option rather than a later conversion flyer. (Wikipedia's two-cabinet framing agrees.)

### Gaps
- No separate Capcom USA flyer for a six-button conversion kit appears in TAFA or KLOV's list. A kit flyer may exist outside TAFA; this is unverified.
- The painter of the cover illustration is not credited on the flyer, and I found no source naming them.
- The LaunchBox games database reportedly lists a North American flyer image, per the search summary (https://gamesdb.launchbox-app.com/games/details/7567). I did not open it, so I could not compare it with A and B.

## European flyers and distributors

### Takeaway
In the UK the game was handled by **Electrocoin** (Electrocoin Automatics / Electrocoin Sales, London NW10). Electrocoin made at least three Street Fighter flyers of its own, plus two or three catalogue flyers. These reuse Capcom's painted Ryu and the flame logo but add Electrocoin's own branding, typography and slogans. TAFA shows no German or Italian Street Fighter flyers.

### Cited Findings
- **Flyer C (UK):** a landscape sheet that is mostly the flame-brush logo, very large, over a crop of the same painted flames and Ryu's fist and gi from Capcom's cover. Under it are "CAPCOM" in a blue outline serif, a black band with a white italic serif slogan calling it a sensational new martial-arts game, and "ELECTROCOIN SALES" in Electrocoin's square stencil-like display face. The Electrocoin "Expanding the World of Play" roundel sits top left. The footer gives Electrocoin House, 180/181 Park Avenue, London NW10 7XH — [TAFA 4007 scan](https://flyers.arcade-museum.com/videogame-flyers/4/street-fighter-04002-01.jpg); [entry](https://flyers.arcade-museum.com/videogames/show/4007)
- **Flyer D (UK):** a sky-blue landscape sheet. It has a cutout photo of the curved US-style deluxe cabinet (rainbow side art, cartoon figures on the panel's front edge, red pads), the flame logo, a large cutout of the painted Ryu bust (red hair, headband, open gi) and three screenshots. The black bold italic headline claims it is the most sensational upright video game of 1987 — [TAFA 4008 scan](https://flyers.arcade-museum.com/videogame-flyers/4/street-fighter-04003-01.jpg)
- **Flyer E (UK) "NOW ON SALE":** a pale green portrait sheet with a red starburst at top left and a grid of eight screenshots, including the bonus brick stage, the Great Wall, a Chinatown "Money Exchanger" stage and a photo of Japanese players. It shows **two cabinets**. On the left is a Japanese-style dedicated cabinet with a painted marquee (a fighter and the flame logo), a red curved "CAPCOM" control-panel fascia, "CAPCOM" on the side and painted side art of a fighter with the logo. On the right is the US-style curved rainbow cabinet. The slogan box calls it the most amazing dedicated upright ever — [TAFA 4009 scan](https://flyers.arcade-museum.com/videogame-flyers/4/street-fighter-04004-01.jpg)
- **Flyer F (1987, "Electrocoin Sales — the expert team…"):** a catalogue sheet. Street Fighter appears as a photo of the curved rainbow cabinet, captioned "CAPCOM STREET FIGHTER — The most amazing dedicated upright ever". It sits alongside Darius, PlayChoice-10, Rampage, WEC Le Mans, Diamond Derby, Bar-X and Bally pinballs, with portrait photos of the five sales staff. TAFA files it under Taito, source Andy Welburn — [TAFA 3977 scan](https://flyers.arcade-museum.com/videogame-flyers/3/darius-street-fighter-diamond-derby-rampage-playch-03972-01.jpg); [entry](https://flyers.arcade-museum.com/videogames/show/3977)
- **Flyer G (1988):** an Electrocoin catalogue that shows, under "CAPCOM VIDEO GAMES", a **different, conventional straight upright**. It has a Street Fighter marquee, about six white buttons per player on a dark panel, and bold red/yellow side art with a "CAPCOM" panel and a fighter figure. It appears with Xenophobe, Lost World, WEC Le Mans and AWPs — [TAFA 3959 scan](https://flyers.arcade-museum.com/videogame-flyers/3/street-fighter-xenophobe-03954-01.jpg)
- **UK marquee (J):** a marquee marked as made under licence by Electrocoin (P.O. Box 1EY, Tottenham Court Road, London). It has a yellow field inside a red border of white stars, a chunky cartoon fighter with a blond quiff in a blue gi and yellow belt doing a flying kick, the flame logo flattened to red and pink, a joystick direction chart, and small figures labelled "Psycho Fire", "Hurricane Kick" and "Dragon Punch". The text tells players to discover secret techniques — [KLOV image 48902](https://www.arcade-museum.com/images-game/48/street-fighter-48902.jpg)

### Inferences
- Electrocoin's names for the special moves, "Psycho Fire" for the Hadoken, alongside "Hurricane Kick" and "Dragon Punch", may be an early Western naming. It appears on a UK marquee only. This comes from one artefact, so treat it as uncertain.
- The straight six-button upright in the 1988 flyer (G), and the cartoonish marquee (J), suggest a UK-built or UK-dressed cabinet for the button version. This is not confirmed.
- Electrocoin's design language was conventional trade-catalogue: cutout cabinet photos, boxed captions in bold sans, its own square display logo and sales-team head shots. It borrowed Capcom's painted Ryu and logo rather than commissioning new illustration.

### Gaps
- I found no German (e.g. NSM/Löwen, Nova), Italian or French distributor flyers in TAFA's list. I did not search other archives.
- I did not view the image for flyer H (TAFA 4019).

## Cabinet side art, marquee, bezel, CPO, instruction cards, manual

### Takeaway
The US dedicated cabinet is the distinctive curved "C-shaped" body. Its side art is an arc of red/orange/yellow/grey stripes ("rainbow" speed lines) with flat cartoon Ken (red gi) and Ryu (blue gi) in kicking and punching poses and a "CAPCOM" plate. The six-button marquee puts the flame-brush logo over the same stripes with cartoon fighters. Both CPOs (pad and button) repeat the stripes and cartoon figures around a gold instruction card.

### Cited Findings
- **Side art (K):** a white curved side panel carrying a wide arc of parallel stripes in yellow, orange, red, pink and grey. Two stacked Ken figures (blond, red gi, black belt) punch and kick at the top, two Ryu figures (brown hair, blue gi) kick lower down, and a "CAPCOM" plate in red serif on dark grey sits between them. The panel's front skirt repeats a frieze of small cartoon fighters on the stripes — [KLOV 48898](https://www.arcade-museum.com/images-game/48/street-fighter-48898.jpg)
- **Marquee (I), US:** a black ground with horizontal stripes in magenta, red, orange and yellow, and the flame-brush STREET FIGHTER logo in a red-to-yellow gradient with dark outline. "CAPCOM" sits in a red-bordered box. Cartoon fighters with flat colours and black ink lines are grouped in pairs on each side: Ryu (blue gi, headband) kicking, Ken (red gi) and Sagat (eyepatch, white shorts), Joe (red trousers, taped fists), Retsu-like (bald, dark gi), Mike-like (vest) and others. One KLOV photo is the glass version with a printed "INSTRUCTIONS" card at right. That card mentions selecting a country with the joystick, the buy-in feature, hitting the button harder for more damage, secret techniques and best-of-3 rounds — [KLOV 34609](https://www.arcade-museum.com/images-game/34/street-fighter-34609.jpg); [KLOV 48904](https://www.arcade-museum.com/images-game/48/street-fighter-48904.jpg)
- **CPO, pad version (L):** a granite-grey laminate panel with two black plates, each holding a ball-top joystick and two large round rubber pads (red/blue). The art band has the same stripes, and the background splits teal (player 1) / red (player 2). Cartoon figures show Ryu (teal gi) kicking Joe, and Birdie (mohawk, vest) facing Ken (red gi) mid-kick. A gold "INSTRUCTIONS" card sits in the centre, with small yellow "PUNCH" and "KICK" tags pointing to the pads. The attract screen in the same photo reads "© CAPCOM 1987" — [KLOV 48906](https://www.arcade-museum.com/images-game/48/street-fighter-48906.jpg)
- **CPO, button version (L):** the same curved cabinet and the same upper art strip with the instruction card. The player plates are painted sky-blue (P1) and red (P2), each with a bat-top stick and six buttons in blue/white/red pairs under PUNCH and KICK headings — [KLOV 48896](https://www.arcade-museum.com/images-game/48/street-fighter-48896.jpg)
- KLOV notes that dedicated cabinets used pneumatic controls connected to the board by air hoses, which "wore out or would constantly require adjustment". It lists the controls as an 8-way joystick plus six buttons (Jab, Strong, Fierce Punch; Short, Forward, Roundhouse Kick) and conversion class JAMMA — [KLOV game page](https://www.arcade-museum.com/Videogame/street-fighter)
- A serial sticker inside a cabinet reads "CAPCOM STREET FIGHTER SERIAL # 0412" — [KLOV 48899](https://www.arcade-museum.com/images-game/48/street-fighter-48899.jpg)
- Flyer photos show the deluxe cabinet's own topper as "STREET FIGHTER" in blue/white outlined letters with small figures, not the flame logo — [TAFA 4390 back](https://flyers.arcade-museum.com/videogame-flyers/4/street-fighter-1943-the-battle-of-midway-bionic-co-04385-02.jpg); [TAFA 1037 back](https://flyers.arcade-museum.com/videogame-flyers/1/street-fighter-01037-02.jpg) (seen at small size; detail uncertain)
- The operator's manual is listed as "Operator's Manual with Illustrated Parts Lists", 56 pages — [KLOV game page](https://www.arcade-museum.com/Videogame/street-fighter)

### Inferences
- The US cabinet art (cartoon cel figures, rainbow speed stripes, Ken in red and Ryu in blue) looks like a coherent Capcom USA package distinct from the Japanese painted art. The Japanese cabinet in flyer E has painted side art and marquee. Ryu's colour changes (blue on the side art and marquee, teal on the pad CPO, white in-game and in the flyers) suggest the cabinet illustrator worked from sketches or a palette brief rather than the sprites.
- A curved cabinet fitted with six buttons (48896, 48898) could be a factory variant or an operator retrofit after pad failures. KLOV's note about pads wearing out makes retrofits plausible. Unconfirmed.
- The marquee instruction card's wording ("hitting the button harder…") reads as written for pressure-sensitive controls.

### Gaps
- No standalone bezel art was found. KLOV has no bezel images.
- I did not open the manual PDF, so its cover design is not described.
- Collector-forum and auction descriptions of reproduction art kits were not searched.

## The Western title logo vs the Japanese one

### Takeaway
The Western pieces use the same Capcom flame/brush STREET FIGHTER wordmark as the international flyer: jagged, torn-edged brush letters in an orange-red to yellow gradient with a heavy black drop shadow, the "S" swooping and the letters slanting. The US marquee redraws it flatter (red-to-yellow, thin dark keyline, no deep shadow), and the UK Electrocoin marquee flattens it to red and pink. I could not view a Japanese domestic flyer to confirm differences.

### Cited Findings
- The same flame-brush wordmark appears on the Japan-printed English flyer, on all three Electrocoin flyers and on the US marquee — [TAFA 1037](https://flyers.arcade-museum.com/videogame-flyers/1/street-fighter-01037-01.jpg); [TAFA 4007](https://flyers.arcade-museum.com/videogame-flyers/4/street-fighter-04002-01.jpg); [KLOV 34609](https://www.arcade-museum.com/images-game/34/street-fighter-34609.jpg)
- A logo-history article describes the series' wordmarks as consistently sketchy, jagged and rough-edged — [Fabrik Brands](https://fabrikbrands.com/branding-matters/logofile/street-fighter-logo-history/) (secondary, general)
- LaunchBox lists a Japanese flyer and a Japanese marquee image, which could be used for comparison — [LaunchBox](https://gamesdb.launchbox-app.com/games/details/7567) (seen only in a search summary)

### Inferences
- There seems to be no separate "Western logo": the flame-brush mark is Capcom's international mark. The Western variation is in rendering (flat-colour signage vs painted gradient) and in the blue/white block-letter topper on the US deluxe cabinet.

### Gaps
- No side-by-side comparison with a Japanese domestic flyer or marquee was done.
