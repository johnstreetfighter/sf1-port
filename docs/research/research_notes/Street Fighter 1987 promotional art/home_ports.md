# Street Fighter (1987): home-port box art and print advertising (1988-1991)

Method note: MobyGames, LemonAmiga, StrategyWiki and NEC Retro refused automated fetches (403 / bot wall), so they could not be checked. The image descriptions below come from scans I downloaded and looked at myself. The scans are from Spectrum Computing (ZXDB), C64-Wiki, TheGamesDB, VGCollect and the LaunchBox Games DB. Where an image is described, the description is my own observation of the scan the URL points at.

## Fighting Street (PC Engine CD-ROM², 1988) and TurboGrafx-CD (US, 1989): cover art, artist, technique, composition

### Takeaway
The Japanese and American discs both use one painted illustration: a red-haired Ryu in a guard stance in front of a flame-tinted Mount Rushmore, with smaller opponents at the sides. The Japanese sleeve shows it full-bleed in a big circle under a hand-brushed red/yellow logo. NEC's US release crops it into the standard white-framed TurboGrafx template with new lettering. Takayuki (Ryukow) Masuo is credited with the cover painting, but the only source is a single fan-database entry that cites the artist's own tweet.

### Cited Findings
- **Japanese front (PC Engine CD-ROM²).**
  - Square jewel-case insert, white ground, "HE system" logo top left and the "CD-ROM² SYSTEM / PC Engine" mark top right.
  - A large circle with a thin red rim holds a painted scene:
    - Ryu, waist-up and centre, faces the viewer with both fists raised. He has bright orange-red curly hair, a blue-white headband, a sleeveless blue-white gi top and red wristbands.
    - Behind him is Mount Rushmore (the US stage backdrop), washed in orange/yellow flame-like light.
    - Small background figures stand on either side: a masked fighter at left (Geki-like) and a bare-chested brawler at right (Joe- or Mike-like).
    - A blue-white energy streak sweeps across the lower right.
  - The logo "FIGHTING STREET" runs in two staggered lines of jagged, dry-brush capitals with a red-to-yellow vertical gradient and black outline, in the manner of the arcade Street Fighter logo. A small katakana "ファイティング・ストリート" sits above it. The logo breaks out of the circle at the bottom.
  - Technique: an opaque painted illustration (gouache/acrylic look) with warm hot-orange and cool-blue contrast. My own observation of the scan. — [TheGamesDB front scan](https://cdn.thegamesdb.net/images/original/boxart/front/93991-1.jpg) (entry: [TheGamesDB #93991](https://thegamesdb.net/game.php?id=93991), region NTSC-J)
- **Japanese back insert.**
  - A generic CD-ROM² system explainer: a blue ring around Japanese text, with a photo of the PC Engine plus CD unit and controller.
  - Hudson Soft bee mascot and HUDSON SOFT logo; "© 1988 HUDSON SOFT, © 1987 CAPCOM".
  - Catalogue number **HCD8002** on the spine side.
  - So the Japanese publisher on the package is Hudson Soft, not NEC Avenue as the brief supposed. — [TheGamesDB back scan](https://cdn.thegamesdb.net/images/original/boxart/back/93991-1.jpg)
- **Cover artist.** vgdensetsu.net's page on Takayuki "Ryukow" Masuo lists "Cover art" for Fighting Street (PC Engine CD, 1988). It gives the source as a mention by the artist on Twitter. — [vgdensetsu.net: Takayuki Masuo](https://vgdensetsu.net/?p=1585) (single fan source; not corroborated)
- **US front (TurboGrafx-CD).**
  - NEC's US house layout: a white/grey dotted frame with "TurboGrafx CD" top left and "ACTION" top right, plus the NEC logo bottom left and a TurboGrafx badge bottom right.
  - The same Masuo painting is cropped to a rectangle: Ryu and the Mount Rushmore faces in flame are visible.
  - The title is redrawn as "FIGHTING STREET" in arched, outlined gold/yellow capitals across the top of the picture. It is not the Japanese brush logo.
  - A round gold seal sits over the left edge of the art.
  - My observation of a low-resolution (200 px) scan. — [VGCollect front](https://vgcollect.com/images/front-box-art/13982.jpg); [VGCollect back](https://vgcollect.com/images/back-box-art/13982.jpg); [VGCollect entry](https://vgcollect.com/item/13982)
- **US release data.** NEC; item number TGXCD1001; barcode 092218004840; 1989. VGCollect credits Alfa System as developer. The back-of-box text names the twelve fighters by country. — [VGCollect](https://vgcollect.com/item/13982)
- **Mount Rushmore on the cover.** A user review on GameFAQs remarks that the Mount Rushmore backdrop was prominent enough to appear on the TurboGrafx-CD cover. — [GameFAQs review (via search result)](https://gamefaqs.gamespot.com/turbocd/589291-fighting-street/reviews/175847)
- **Critical reception of the cover.** GamesRadar's "Awful Box Art: Street Fighter series" includes the TurboGrafx-CD Fighting Street cover. This is known only from a search snippet; the article body would not load. — [GamesRadar](https://www.gamesradar.com/awful-box-art-street-fighter-series)
- **Launch timing.** NEC Retro's list dates the US Fighting Street release to 1989-11 (via a search summary, not fetched directly). The tg-16.com archive's note on Gekkan PC Engine #1 says No·Ri·Ko and Fighting Street were the only CD-ROM² titles available at the time. — [tg-16.com archive](https://archives.tg-16.com/Gekkan_PC_Engine_1989_01.htm)

### Inferences
- The painting puts the US stage (Mount Rushmore) behind a Japanese hero. It reads as a "world tour" image, and it would have needed no change for the US market.
- The Japanese logo deliberately echoes the arcade's brush-lettered Street Fighter logo, since the game had to be renamed. The US repackaging drops that link in favour of NEC's generic template lettering.
- Ryu's red hair matches his original 1987 arcade portrait, not the later brown or black hair.

### Gaps
- No US print ad specifically for Fighting Street was found. TurboGrafx-CD launch ads ran in EGM and elsewhere in late 1989, but no scan featuring Fighting Street was located. Next places to look: the tg-16.com magazine archive and EGM/TurboPlay scans on archive.org.
- No Japanese magazine ad for Fighting Street was located, though Gekkan PC Engine and Marukatsu PC Engine 1988-89 scans would be the place to look.
- The Masuo credit rests on one fan database citing a tweet. I did not see the tweet.
- I could not confirm whether NEC Avenue had any role. The package itself names Hudson Soft.

## Tiertex ports for U.S. Gold / Go! (1988): Amiga, Atari ST, C64, Spectrum, CPC, MS-DOS; covers, artists, variants, budget and compilation re-releases

### Takeaway
Europe got one painted cover used on every format. In a night-time alley, a long-haired hero is seen from behind in a brown leather jacket with "STREET FIGHTER" daubed on the back and a chain over his shoulder. He faces a line of six thugs: one with a baseball bat, a headband-wearing martial artist, and a mohawked punk in sunglasses holding a nunchaku. The arcade brush logo sits above, and the whole thing is set in Capcom's purple/blue striped "Giants of the video games industry" trade dress. The US Capcom USA release (Amiga at least) used entirely different art: a gi-clad silhouette in a smoky, backlit doorway on black, with yellow lettering. No cover artist is credited in any database I could reach.

### Cited Findings
- **Go! (UK) Spectrum inlay** (double-jewel-case, fold-out):
  - **Front artwork.** The hero stands centre foreground in a brown leather jacket with "STREET FIGHTER" brushed on in white and a chain over the shoulder. Six opponents in leather and denim fan out in front of a chain-link fence and city towers at night. Litter (cans, rubbish bins) lies on wet ground.
  - **Front text and badges.** The arcade logo (red-to-yellow brush capitals) is at top left. A red-and-yellow Sinclair User "10/10 Classic" roundel is at top right. A strapline under the picture reads "THE COMPUTER VERSION OF THE CLASSIC COIN-OP", then a red "SPECTRUM 48/128, +2 Cassette" bar, a big red outlined "CAPCOM" box and "GIANTS OF THE VIDEO GAMES INDUSTRY".
  - **Background and spine.** The background is horizontal purple, magenta and blue bands. The spine reads "STREET FIGHTER" in white serif capitals on blue.
  - **Inner panels.** The back blurb (English, with French, German and Italian on other panels) uses the tagline "...LET NOTHING STAND IN YOUR WAY". It shows four screenshots labelled CBM 64, Spectrum, Amstrad and Atari ST.
  - **Capcom Passport promotion.** The inlay carries a "Capcom Passport" promotion: "Win the trip of a lifetime" to Silicon Valley, with a Boeing 747 photo. The Passport voucher lists Bionic Commando, Street Fighter, 1943, L.E.D. Storm, Black Tiger, Tiger Road, Last Duel and Forgotten Worlds. A cut-out "Ticket to America" coupon is printed in the corner.
  - **Imprint.** © 1987 Capcom Co. Ltd. Manufactured and distributed by Go! Media Holdings Ltd., a division of U.S. Gold, Holford, Birmingham.
  - Source: my observation of the scan. — [Spectrum Computing inlay scan](https://spectrumcomputing.co.uk/zxdb/sinclair/entries/0009425/StreetFighter.jpg)
- **ZXDB entry (Spectrum).**
  - Release: Go! (UK) 1988 at £8.99 cassette, £12.99 disk.
  - Credits: John Prince, Duncan Campbell and Martin Wakeley of Tiertex, plus Leigh Christian (Blue Turtle) with no stated role. No cover artist is listed.
  - Re-releases: Erbe Software (Spain, 1988, 875 Ptas) and Kixx (UK).
  - Compilations: 2 Por 1 (Road Blasters + Street Fighter, Erbe 1988), Vitaminas (Erbe 1989), Arcade Muscle (U.S. Gold 1989), Multimixx 3 (Kixx 1991) and 10 Pack (Gremlin Graphics 1991).
  - Other appearances: a demo on the Sinclair User #74 Megatape 3 (May 1988), and an unauthorised Italian Load 'n' Run #55 version titled "Arti marziali".
  - Source: [Spectrum Computing entry 9425](https://spectrumcomputing.co.uk/entry/9425)
- **Erbe (Spain) re-release.** Reuses the Go! front art and trade dress, including the 10/10 roundel, under a "SPECTRUM" header. The spine is red with "STREET FIGHTER" and Spanish blurb, the catalogue code is SE 434, and the ERBE logo appears twice. — [Spectrum Computing Erbe inlay](https://spectrumcomputing.co.uk/pub/sinclair/games-inlays/Rereleases/s/StreetFighter(ErbeSoftwareS.A.).jpg)
- **Kixx (budget) Spectrum inlay.**
  - Layout: the same painting is cropped tighter and tilted inside a frame with a black zigzag edge. The background is Kixx's pink-and-blue diamond lattice. A yellow header reads "SPECTRUM 48/128,+2", with a tilted "LET NOTHING STAND IN YOUR WAY...." banner over the art. A big pink-and-yellow KIXX lozenge sits at the bottom between a Union Jack and an Italian flag.
  - Back: press quotes from CPC Computing, Zzap and Crash, plus three screenshots, on yellow.
  - Licensing line: "Licensed to KIXX".
  - Instructions name the opponents "Retsu and Geki, Joe and Mike, Birdie and Eagle, Lee and Gen, Adon and Sagat".
  - A second, dark-blue Kixx variant also exists.
  - Sources: [Kixx inlay](https://spectrumcomputing.co.uk/zxdb/sinclair/entries/0009425/StreetFighter(Kixx).jpg); [Kixx dark-blue variant](https://spectrumcomputing.co.uk/zxdb/sinclair/entries/0009425/StreetFighter(Kixx)_2.jpg); [Kixx back](https://spectrumcomputing.co.uk/zxdb/sinclair/entries/0009425/StreetFighter(Kixx)_Back.jpg)
- **Commodore 64.**
  - C64-Wiki shows two European covers:
    - A Go! "CBM 64/128 CASSETTE" tape inlay with the same painting, Capcom trade dress and strapline, but no 10/10 roundel.
    - A Kixx "CBM 64/128" cover with the pink lattice and zigzag frame, and UK, German and Italian flags.
  - Publisher and developer: Go! (Tiertex), on disk and tape.
  - Note: the wiki says "two Street Fighter versions" could be bought together, one on each side of a disk or tape, but it does not explain what the two versions were.
  - Sources: [C64-Wiki page](https://www.c64-wiki.com/wiki/Street_Fighter_(Go!)); [Kixx cover scan](https://www.c64-wiki.com/images/6/66/streetfighter_eu_coverf.jpg); [Go! tape cover scan](https://www.c64-wiki.com/images/b/b2/streetfighter_eu_tapecoverf.jpg)
- **Amiga, European release** (U.S. Gold/Go!, 1988):
  - The box front reproduces the magazine-ad layout: the arcade logo, the "....NOTHING STANDS IN YOUR WAY" headline, the painting, and a panel of screenshots with prices.
  - The back has a red-and-white jagged starburst border on dark blue, with multilingual blurb and screenshots.
  - The **Kixx Amiga** re-release puts the painting in a red/white starburst frame under the arcade logo, with a "CAPCOM USA" box, the Kixx logo and UK, German and Italian flags, labelled "CBM AMIGA".
  - Source: my observation of LaunchBox scans. — [LaunchBox Amiga entry](https://gamesdb.launchbox-app.com/games/details/73254-street-fighter)
- **Amiga, US release (Capcom USA).**
  - Front: an entirely different cover on a black ground. A lone figure in a martial-arts gi, shown as a dark silhouette from behind or front, stands in a narrow corridor or doorway with strong backlight and drifting smoke or steam. It looks photographic or airbrushed rather than painted; I could not determine which.
  - Title: "STREET FIGHTER" in two lines of chunky, slightly distressed yellow capitals.
  - Branding: a red "CAPCOM USA" box at the foot and a red "Amiga 512K" format tag at bottom left.
  - Back: black, with the yellow title and the tagline "So you think you're mean enough?". The copy mentions "Eight bad dudes" and gives the Capcom U.S.A. address at 3303 Scott Boulevard, Santa Clara.
  - Disk label: white, with a red "STREET FIGHTER" title and a red CAPCOM USA box.
  - Source: my observation of LaunchBox scans, at low resolution. — [LaunchBox Amiga entry](https://gamesdb.launchbox-app.com/games/details/73254-street-fighter)
- **Atari ST.** Atarimania lists:
  - Publisher: US Gold, Europe 1988; developer Tiertex. Programmer Gordon Fong, musician Wally Beben. No cover artist is listed and no box scan is shown.
  - Re-releases: Go! France (1989) and Kixx (Europe, 1991).
  - Advertising: 11 ad scans, including a UK ad.
  - Coverage: a 43% review in ST Action (October 1988), and a June 1988 U.S. Gold feature in ST Action.
  - Source: [Atarimania ST Street Fighter](https://www.atarimania.com/game-atari-st-street-fighter_11416.html); [UK ad thumbnail](https://www.atarimania.com/pubs/low_res/atari-st-ad-street-fighter-uk.jpg)
- **MS-DOS (conflicting sources).**
  - StrategyWiki (via search snippet) credits a Hi-Tech Expressions DOS port, sold mainly in the US. It says the box art resembles contemporary Capcom NES boxes.
  - A Capcom fandom wiki says Micro Talent developed it and Capcom USA published it.
  - A personal blog credits a "James L. Hanson of Capcom".
  - The brief's assumption of a Tiertex DOS port is also asserted by some summaries.
  - None of this could be verified. — [StrategyWiki Versions (403 to fetcher)](https://strategywiki.org/wiki/Street_Fighter/Versions); [Capcom fandom](https://capcom.fandom.com/wiki/File:SF_Game_Video.png); [lilura1 blog](https://lilura1.blogspot.com/2022/06/Street-Fighter-Games-MS-DOS.html?m=0)
- **Amstrad CPC.** The European release used the same Go! art; the ad lists Amstrad at £9.99 tape / £14.99 disk. A LaunchBox CPC entry exists, but I did not inspect its scans. My CPC-Power fetch landed on an unrelated game ID, so its jaquette page was not checked. — [LaunchBox CPC entry](https://gamesdb.launchbox-app.com/games/details/109583-street-fighter)

### Inferences
- The European painting is a Western reinterpretation, not Capcom artwork:
  - The hero, presumably Ryu, wears a leather biker jacket instead of a gi.
  - The opponents are generic street toughs, not the arcade's twelve.
  - The "STREET FIGHTER" painted on the jacket is literal and graffiti-like.
- The European painting's style is typical UK publisher cover painting of 1987-88: realistic figures, dramatic low key light, and a cool blue/violet night palette set against the warm brown jacket. This is my stylistic judgement, not sourced.
- Its compositional device is the "lone hero facing the gang", with the hero seen from behind so the player identifies with him.
- Its trade dress (purple/blue band stripes, red outlined CAPCOM box, "GIANTS OF THE VIDEO GAMES INDUSTRY") appears to be the shared look of U.S. Gold/Go!'s 1988 Capcom line, as the Passport promotion ties the titles together. This is inferred from this one inlay, not verified on the sibling titles.
- On Capcom's US line (Amiga, and probably DOS and C64): Capcom USA commissioned its own minimal, moody cover rather than licensing the UK painting.

### Gaps
- **No cover artist credit** for the European painting was found in ZXDB, C64-Wiki, Atarimania or a Spectrum Computing cover-artists forum thread. Leigh Christian (Blue Turtle) is credited on ZXDB without a role, so it cannot be assumed he painted it.
- **The Capcom USA cover's artist and technique** are unknown.
- **MS-DOS cover(s) not seen.** The developer and publisher are contested, as listed above.
- **Compilation covers not viewed:** Arcade Muscle (U.S. Gold 1989), Multimixx 3 (Kixx 1991), 10 Pack (Gremlin 1991), 2 Por 1 and Vitaminas (Erbe). Their exact contents and art were not verified.
- **Pages that blocked the fetcher:** MobyGames' cover gallery (which would settle regional variants) and LemonAmiga.

## Print ads (Crash, Your Sinclair, Zzap!64, Amstrad Action, ACE, C+VG, TGM, etc.)

### Takeaway
Go!/U.S. Gold ran one multi-format campaign from July 1988 into early 1989, as a two-page spread and a single-page version. Both reuse the box painting under the arcade logo and a "....NOTHING STANDS IN YOUR WAY" headline, with screenshot strips, review quotes and a price grid. ZXDB indexes 25 placements across ACE, C&VG, Crash, Popular Computing Weekly, Sinclair User, The Games Machine and Your Sinclair.

### Cited Findings
- **Placements indexed by ZXDB** (all for the Go! Street Fighter ad):
  - ACE #11 (Aug 1988, p. 50, two pages), #14 (p. 48), #15 (p. 97), #17 (p. 44), #18 (Mar 1989, p. 44).
  - C&VG #81 (Jul 1988, p. 6, two pages), #82 (p. 116, full page), #85, #86, #87, #88 (Feb 1989, p. 2).
  - Crash #54 (Jul 1988, p. 2, two pages), #55 (p. 4, full page), #58 (p. 79), #59 (p. 147).

  - ACE PDFs are linked on archive.org.
  - Source: [Spectrum Computing entry 9425](https://spectrumcomputing.co.uk/entry/9425)
- **Two-page spread.**
  - The painting runs across the spread. The arcade logo is top left, and "....NOTHING STANDS IN YOUR WAY" runs across the top in white italic condensed capitals on a purple-striped band.
  - A right-hand column holds four screenshots (Spectrum, Amstrad, CBM 64/128 and Atari ST).
  - Copy and quotes: "EXPLOSIVE COIN-OP ACTION!", a Sinclair User "Sinclair Classic" quote likening it favourably to Renegade, and a Your Sinclair quote.
  - Prices: CBM 64/128 £9.99t/£14.99d; Spectrum 48K £8.99t; Spectrum +3 £12.99d; Amstrad £9.99t/£14.99d; Atari ST £19.99d; Amiga £24.99d.
  - Footer: the red CAPCOM box with "Giants of the video games industry" and Go! Media Holdings' Birmingham address. A small "Capcom U.S.A., Inc. © 1987" sits at the bottom right.
  - Source: my observation. — [Spectrum Computing advert scan 2](https://spectrumcomputing.co.uk/pub/sinclair/games-adverts/s/StreetFighter_2.jpg)
- **Single-page version.** The same elements are restacked: the painting on top, then a band of four screenshots (Spectrum, CBM 64/128, Amstrad, Atari ST) around the copy block and price list, with the Capcom box at the foot. — [Spectrum Computing advert scan 1](https://spectrumcomputing.co.uk/pub/sinclair/games-adverts/s/StreetFighter.jpg)
- **C64-Wiki** also hosts an advert scan for the C64 version. — [C64-Wiki advert thumbnail](https://www.c64-wiki.com/images/thumb/f/f4/streetfighter_werbung.jpg/400px-streetfighter_werbung.jpg)
- **Atarimania** holds 11 ST ad thumbnails, including a UK one. — [Atarimania](https://www.atarimania.com/game-atari-st-street-fighter_11416.html)
- **Review placements next to the ads** (useful for dating):
  - Sinclair User #74 (May 1988), 10/10 "Classic", which is the source of the cover roundel.
  - Your Sinclair #31: 8/10. Crash #53: 69%. TGM #9: 60%. ACE #11: 410/1000. ST Action (Oct 1988): 43%.
  - Sources: [Spectrum Computing](https://spectrumcomputing.co.uk/entry/9425); [Atarimania](https://www.atarimania.com/game-atari-st-street-fighter_11416.html)

### Inferences
- The ads launched in July 1988 issues with quotes from May-July reviews. The Spectrum inlay's 10/10 roundel shows the inlay was printed or reprinted after the Sinclair User review.
- One painting served as box, ad and Kixx budget art for about three years (1988-1991), a typical cost-saving reuse.
- The campaign's visual language:
  - the arcade logo as the brand anchor;
  - a painted night-city gang scene;
  - italic all-caps headline;
  - purple/blue striped Capcom band;
  - real screenshots per format, labelled.
  - It sells "coin-op authenticity" through the logo and screenshots, and "attitude" through the painting.

### Gaps
- **Ads in Zzap!64 and Amstrad Action** are not indexed by ZXDB, so they were not confirmed. They likely exist given the multi-format campaign, but this is unverified. Next: archive.org Zzap!64 #39-41 and Amstrad Action #34-36 (mid-1988).
- **US Capcom USA ads** for the Amiga, C64 or DOS versions were not found.
- **Erbe (Spain) ads in MicroHobby** were not checked.
