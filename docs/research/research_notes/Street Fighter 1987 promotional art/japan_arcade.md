# Japanese promotional and official artwork for Street Fighter (Capcom arcade, 1987)

Scope: the 1987 arcade original and its 1987-1988 Japanese promotion only. Researcher note: about 20 tool calls. Several key sources were unreachable: the Street Fighter Fandom wiki returned HTTP 402, wiki.arcadeotaku.com blocked the fetch with a bot wall, and exotica.org.uk sent a browser-verification page. Image files could not be viewed visually; only their page metadata could be read. **No description of the Japanese flyer's imagery below is verified first-hand.** What is verified: the cabinet and version facts, the staff credits, the chart and award data, and the flyer-archive inventory.

## Japanese arcade flyers (チラシ): how many versions, and what do they show?

### Takeaway
Japan sold two distinct products, and each would plausibly have had its own sales flyer: the デラックスアップライト (pressure-pad upright, end of August 1987) and the テーブル スタンダードタイプ (six-button cocktail table, end of October 1987). I could not find or view a scan of either Japanese flyer. The Arcade Flyer Archive listings I could open were EU and UK flyers only.

### Cited Findings
- Japanese Wikipedia says the upright launched at the end of August 1987 under the name "デラックスアップライト". It had one lever and two large pressure-sensitive buttons per player, and the cabinet was built by Atari Games after Capcom's in-house cabinet development failed. — [ja.wikipedia: ストリートファイター (ゲーム)](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/ストリートファイター_(ゲーム))
- The same source says the table version launched at the end of October 1987 as the "スタンダードタイプ", with one lever and six buttons (light, medium and strong punch and kick). The pressure cabinet earned less than expected at location tests, so Capcom moved to six buttons, and that version topped the trade paper ゲームマシン's ranking. — [ja.wikipedia](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/ストリートファイター_(ゲーム))
- Famitsu (2022 anniversary piece) gives the arcade start date as 1987年8月30日. It says Japan had two versions, an アップライト筐体 with pressure-sensing buttons (players stood) and a テーブル筐体 with six buttons. — [Famitsu](https://www.famitsu.com/news/202208/30273592.html)
- English Wikipedia says the Regular six-button version was a 26-inch cocktail (table) cabinet in Japan and an upright overseas. The Deluxe version had two pneumatic pressure pads. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Street_Fighter_(video_game))
- English Wikipedia (as summarized in a search result) says the flyers show a closed fist striking the pads, whereas Capcom staff and Japanese magazine editors said the correct method was an open palm. Gamest editor-in-chief Zenji Ishii is quoted on how to press the pads. This is about flyer imagery in general; the region of the flyer in question was not confirmed. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Street_Fighter_(video_game))
- Wikipedia's infobox image is captioned "North American arcade flyer", not a Japanese one. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Street_Fighter_(video_game))
- Arcade Flyer Archive inventory as checked:
  - Listing 1037 is EU (front and back scans; full scans at `/videogame-flyers/1/street-fighter-01037-01.jpg` and `-02.jpg`). — [TAFA 1037](https://flyers.arcade-museum.com/videogames/show/1037)
  - Listing 4008 is a UK "1st alternate" front, with the scan at `/videogame-flyers/4/street-fighter-04003-01.jpg`. — [TAFA 4008](https://flyers.arcade-museum.com/videogames/show/4008)
  - Listing 4009 is labelled UK in search results but was not opened. — [TAFA 4009](https://flyers.arcade-museum.com/videogames/show/4009)
  - No Japan-flagged Street Fighter 1987 listing turned up in the search results.
- arcade-history.com (Gaming History) holds a "Vintage ad" image (`images/admini160/2647_3.jpg`), a cabinet photo and a "goodies/cover" image for the game. All are members-only and none is described. — [arcade-history.com id 2647](https://www.arcade-history.com/?n=street-fighter&page=detail&id=2647)
- The People's GDArchive has an item titled "Street Fighter Arcade Booklet Advertisement". It was not opened, so its region and content are unverified. — [peoplesgdarchive item 13285](https://peoplesgdarchive.org/item/13285/street-fighter-arcade-booklet-advertisement)

### Inferences
- Because the two Japanese products launched about two months apart under different names, Japanese chirashi most likely exist for each: the デラックスアップライト around August 1987 and the テーブル スタンダードタイプ around October 1987. A Japanese flyer for the Deluxe would likely feature the large pressure buttons, since they were the selling point. This is an inference, not seen.
- Overseas flyers (EU, UK, NA) are well archived, but Japanese ones are not on TAFA or at least were not found. Auction records (Yahoo! Auctions Japan, Mandarake) and Japanese collector blogs are the likely places to find them.

### Gaps
- The imagery, technique, palette and lettering of any Japanese flyer could not be verified. No scan was reached and nothing was described from memory.
- Whether a single combined Japanese flyer covers both cabinets is unknown.
- The fist-on-pad flyer claim was not tied to a specific region's flyer.

## Cabinet art, marquee, title logo, instruction cards and operator manual

### Takeaway
No source I reached describes the Japanese cabinet side art, the marquee, the logo designer or the instruction cards. The one solid hardware fact is that Atari Games manufactured the Deluxe upright cabinet.

### Cited Findings
- Atari Games built the pressure-pad upright cabinet. — [ja.wikipedia](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/ストリートファイター_(ゲーム)); also reported by [EventHubs (2013)](https://www.eventhubs.com/news/2013/oct/28/atari-worked-deluxe-version-original-street-fighter-arcade-game/) (not opened)
- The game credits list "ハード・プランニング: くぼぞのたかし (PUNCH KUBOZO)" and "メカトロニクス: STRONG TAKE, RADISH KAMIN". These are the hardware and cabinet staff; the credits name no separate cabinet or graphic artist. — [ja.wikipedia](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/ストリートファイター_(ゲーム)); [arcade-history.com](https://www.arcade-history.com/?n=street-fighter&page=detail&id=2647)
- On the title screen, the brick wall lists the earlier Capcom games Avengers, Commando, Legendary Wings and Trojan. This is in-game title art, not cabinet art. — [arcade-history.com](https://www.arcade-history.com/?n=street-fighter&page=detail&id=2647)

### Inferences
- Because Atari Games built the Deluxe cabinet, its physical cabinet design may have come from Atari's side. Whether the Japanese side art and marquee were Capcom-made or Atari-made is unknown.

### Gaps
- Who designed the logo, and its treatment (brush or chrome): not found.
- Japanese marquee, side art, control-panel overlay and instruction cards: not found.
- Operator manual art: not found. TAFA and the KLOV manual sections were not reached.

## Character illustrations for the 1987 release: who drew them?

### Takeaway
The in-game credits list six pseudonymous キャラクター・デザイン staff. Keiji Inafune, by widely repeated accounts, drew the character portraits (and is said to have fully designed Adon), but he left for Mega Man within about a month. The well-known Bengus (CRMK) paintings of the SF1 cast are later work, mid-1990s or after, and not 1987 promotional art.

### Cited Findings
- Credits as given on Japanese Wikipedia:
  - ディレクション: 西山隆志 (PISTON TAKASHI)
  - プランニング: 松本裕司 (FINISH HIROSHI)
  - プログラミング: こいけひろし (FZ 2151) and すがはらのぶやす (SEINTO SINN)
  - キャラクター・デザイン: CRUSHER ICHI, DABADA ATSUSHI, BONSOIR YUKO, OGAN MIYUKI, BRAVO OYU, INNOCENT SAICHO
  - サウンド&ミュージック: 坂口由洋 (YUUKICHYANS PAPA)

  — [ja.wikipedia](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/ストリートファイター_(ゲーム)). Arcade-history spells the names slightly differently ("Crusher Ighi", "Ocan Miyuki", "Bravo Ovu") and lists special thanks including Short Arm Seigo, Melanin Kazu and Puttsun Midori. — [arcade-history.com](https://www.arcade-history.com/?n=street-fighter&page=detail&id=2647)
- The English Wikipedia infobox credits: Director Takashi Nishiyama, Designer Hiroshi Matsumoto, Programmer Hiroshi Koike, **Artist Keiji Inafune**, Composer Yoshihiro Sakaguchi. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Street_Fighter_(video_game))
- Inafune "got his start" at Capcom designing and illustrating the character portraits for SF1, and based several designs on the manga Karate Baka Ichidai. This is from the Wikipedia summary as returned by search. — [Wikipedia: Keiji Inafune](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keiji_Inafune)
- PC Games (German) says he also made the level-select screens and the fighters' facial expressions. — [PC Games](https://www.pcgames.de/Mega-Man-Thema-205413/Specials/Keiji-Inafune-Der-Mann-der-Mega-Man-gepraegt-und-die-Spielewelt-veraendert-hat-1450544/) (search snippet only)
- Siliconera reports that Inafune moved to Mega Man about a month into Street Fighter. — [Siliconera](https://www.siliconera.com/?p=327011) (search snippet only). EventHubs reports that Adon was the one character he designed fully. — [EventHubs 2013](https://eventhubs.com/news/2013/sep/01/inafune-worked-original-street-fighter-game-start-his-time-capcom-designed-adon) (not opened)
- Ryu and Sagat were partly based on Yoshiji Soeno and Reiba from Karate Baka Ichidai. — [arcade-history.com](https://www.arcade-history.com/?n=street-fighter&page=detail&id=2647). Matsumoto based Adon and Sagat partly on Muay Thai. — [ja.wikipedia](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/ストリートファイター_(ゲーム))
- Fighters Generation says the four Bengus character artworks for SF1 were made after the 1987 release and popularized through art books and guides around 1998-2001. — [Fighters Generation](https://www.fightersgeneration.com/games/sf-p6.html) (fan site)
- According to a search snippet, the Fandom Ryu gallery credits "promotional artwork" of Ryu's SF1 ending to Kiyoshi Utata. The date and whether it was a flyer are unverified, and the page itself returned HTTP 402. — [Fandom Ryu/Gallery](https://streetfighter.fandom.com/wiki/Ryu/Gallery)
- Search results attribute Ryu's character design on Wikipedia to Manabu Takemura. This was not confirmed for 1987 promotional art. — [Wikipedia: Ryu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryu_(Street_Fighter))

### Inferences
- The in-game portraits (the pre-fight and victory faces) are the best-documented 1987 Japanese "official character art", and they are pixel art attributed to Inafune. Any 1987 painted promotional illustration of Ryu or Sagat has no artist credit I could find.
- When sourcing "1987 art", treat Bengus and other 1990s pieces as anachronistic.

### Gaps
- Which real artist sits behind each of the six pseudonyms: not found.
- The Kiyoshi Utata credit comes from a single fan wiki and is undated.
- A shmuplations interview with Nishiyama or Matsumoto about the art was not checked.

## Magazine coverage art: Gamest, Beep!, Game Machine, Micom BASIC (1987-1988)

### Takeaway
There is chart and award data for Game Machine and Gamest. I found no description of specific Japanese magazine ads or feature illustrations.

### Cited Findings
- Game Machine charts: the Deluxe upright reached #3 on the upright/cockpit chart in October 1987. The Japanese cocktail version reached #1 on the table chart in January 1988. The game was listed as the fifth most successful upright in September 1987, and all versions were off the charts by mid-1988. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Street_Fighter_(video_game))
- 1st Gamest Award (1987 season): 7th for Grand Prize, 6th for Best Graphics, 6th for Best Voice Synthesis, and 5th in large-cabinet income. 2nd Gamest Award: 8th for annual hit. — [ja.wikipedia](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/ストリートファイター_(ゲーム)); Wikipedia EN gives the 1987 large-cabinet 5th and 1988 8th rankings. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Street_Fighter_(video_game))
- Later reader polls: 29th in ザ・ベストゲーム (1991); also discussed in ザ・ベストゲーム2 (1998). — [ja.wikipedia](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/ストリートファイター_(ゲーム))
- Related 1988 Japanese media that would carry official art: Pony Canyon gameplay VHS (May 1988, about 20 minutes), and Alfa Records' *Capcom Game Music Vol.2* (28XA-203, March 25, 1988). — [arcade-history.com](https://www.arcade-history.com/?n=street-fighter&page=detail&id=2647)
- Neither Wikipedia article mentions Beep!. — [ja.wikipedia](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/ストリートファイター_(ゲーム)); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Street_Fighter_(video_game))

### Inferences
- The best leads for Japanese ad art are the 1987-1988 issues of Game Machine (scanned at onitama.tv/gamemachine), Gamest's first issues (Gamest started in 1986), and the Capcom Game Music Vol.2 LP/CD sleeve and the Pony Canyon VHS sleeve. None of these was checked.

### Gaps
- No Gamest, Beep!, Micom BASIC or Game Machine ad or feature art was viewed or described.
- The cover art of the soundtrack album and the VHS was not described.
