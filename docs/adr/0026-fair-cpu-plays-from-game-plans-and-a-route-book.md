# The Fair CPU plays from game plans and a generated route book, and owns every CPU command

On 2026-11-18 the maintainer said the Fair CPU is not as active as a top player: it never follows up a hit, drops its intent after every action, and guards as readily as it attacks ([design](../design/2026-11-18-stronger-fair-cpu-design.md)). We decided that the Fair CPU judges which situation the fight is in and follows a game plan, a multi-step script that branches on what it sees, and that its combos come from a route book generated by searching the real engine, with every route proven by a test.

We chose that over two alternatives. Adding more single actions to the existing scorer was cheaper, but the CPU would still forget its intent after each action, so pressure and combo resets would come out by luck of the scores. Having the CPU look ahead in the engine during a fight was stronger on paper, but it costs time every frame, more under rollback and Dramatic Battle, and is hard to scale down across four difficulty levels or to keep looking like a person. The search survives offline only, as what fills the route book. A machine-learned CPU was ruled out: it cannot be tuned by hand or re-run by the recording checker of [ADR-0025](0025-matchmaking-service-accounts-and-trusted-cpu-matches.md).

The fair limits hold at every difficulty level: it never reads inputs, plays through the pad, still makes mistakes, and waits a human reaction delay. That delay now has two values, a fast one for the one event the CPU is watching for (its focus) and a slower one for everything else, because a person reacts at their best only to what they expect. At Very Difficult these are 12 and 17 frames.

This changes [ADR-0019](0019-utility-ai-control-schemes-and-replay-takeover-on-the-1987-engine.md). The Utility AI answered with its own delays, down to one frame at Very Difficult, and teched throws from the engine's tech-window flag, which broke the Fair CPU's promise. The Fair CPU now enters every command in CPU fights and plays the opponent in replay takeover; the Utility AI remains only for Training's scripted drills and combo trials. Under an overhaul preset the 1987 era style is also played by the Fair CPU; only the Faithful 1987 preset keeps Capcom's CPU.

## Consequences

- The route book's content is part of what both sides of a netplay session must agree on.
- A moveset change that breaks a route fails the suite until the route is searched again.
- Every difficulty level plays harder than before. The CPU version number of ADR-0025 is bumped once, when the new play is switched on for the whole cast at the same time.
- Until then the new play sits behind a developer-only switch and the old scorer stays in the build; the old scorer is deleted when the switch goes on for good.
