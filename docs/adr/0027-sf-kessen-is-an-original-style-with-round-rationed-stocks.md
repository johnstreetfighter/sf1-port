# SF KESSEN is an original era style with round-rationed stocks, and its rules follow the fighter they constrain

On 2026-11-18 the maintainer asked for an era style that follows Clayton Chapman's blueprint for a new Street Fighter instead of any Capcom entry ([design](../design/2026-11-18-sf-kessen-era-style-design.md)). Every era style until now stood for a real entry or adapted a fighter to one. We decided that `SF KESSEN` is a family of its own beside `SF2` and `SF6`, built on each fighter's Super Turbo moves, and the first original style: it is shown for a fighter once his kit is built, with or without what-if style options.

We chose that over two alternatives. A revision inside the `SF2` family was cheapest, but a revision delta only adjusts numbers and switches, and this style replaces the meter and adds rules SF2 has no room for. A match-wide ruleset like Definitive Overhaul was closest to the blueprint, which describes one game with one set of rules, but it would stop being an era style: no Kessen Ryu against SF6 Ken, and nothing on the style card.

Two consequences would surprise a later reader.

**A second resource that is not built by fighting.** Versatile stocks are granted at the start of each round and nothing during a round adds one. They are separate from the Super meter, so Unified Gauge and the Team Era Gauge replace only the Super meter and each Kessen fighter keeps his own stocks. No Gauges removes both, and a Kessen fighter then plays as if he held fewer than three stocks and could spend none.

**In a mixed-style match each rule follows the fighter it constrains.** The blueprint's neutral rules were written as rules of a whole game. Here a Kessen fighter suffers proximity guard lag, pays for his wakeup options and keeps his three-stock privileges whoever he faces, while his opponent keeps his own style's rules. A Kessen attack's forced posture and juggle permission apply to any victim. An opponent with no stocks counts as holding three or more wherever a Kessen move checks them, so no move gets its bonus against a fighter who cannot spend. While a Kessen fighter holds three or more stocks, only the attacker's style's super moves can KO him through a block.
