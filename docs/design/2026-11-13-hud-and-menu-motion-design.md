# HUD and menu motion

Design for review · 2026-11-13 · Reference baseline `01bd68c`

## Intent

Make heavy hits, growing combos, gauge readiness and spending, and round finishes easier to feel at a glance. Give menu inputs the same quick feedback. Use the original 384×224 pixel grid, ROM lettering and palette, and the game’s existing layout. The approved reference is the side-by-side *HUD and menu comparison* (`.superpowers/brainstorm/314-1794609355/content/hud-and-menu-combined-preview-v2.html` in the original checkout). It stages HUD events over native captures. The game follows its actual event order and round timing.

The project covers the approved fight HUD and front-end surfaces from Main Menu through local rematch. Keep the original attract and cabinet presentation and the Faithful 1987 fight render path. Preserve input and simulation timing, health and gauge rules, combo counts, announcer cues, screen routing, the native winner line, and the 15-frame cabinet blink. This pass uses the existing art, fonts, settings, and input routes.

## Approaches

1. **Extend existing fight and screen renderers (selected).** `Fight` owns the fight-frame history needed to draw its HUD. Menu screens own the short effects attached to their accepted inputs. `GameSession` clocks front-end presentation and passes arrival cues to the destination screens. This reuses existing event sources and geometry. It requires a small amount of state at each owner and uses shared draw helpers where the drawing is already shared.
2. **Introduce a shared presentation timeline.** Typed events feed one place to inspect and tune effects and their priorities. That would help if the game needed broader choreography. Here it would add event routing, lifetime rules, and reset plumbing for two distinct clocks and duplicate some responsibilities of `GaugeEffects` and `CalloutBoard`.

George selected the first approach and the lifebar trail plus menu cursor as the first playable slice.

## State and rendering

- Update fight cues once per fight step, including during rollback resimulation. Store their minimal transient history in `FightSnapshot` with the existing gauge, combo, call-out, and impact presentation state. Gameplay checksums, replay files, network packets, ROM state, and match configuration exclude the cues. A render is read-only. Undoing a predicted hit or K.O. restores the presentation history too. Replay scrub, bookmark rewind, and spectator catch-up use their normal restore and fight step paths. Clear stale HUD cues on round restart and match reset, and avoid retriggering when a HUD element first appears or returns.
- Drive menu cues from the `GameSession` frame clock, including local Pause when the fight clock is frozen. Start a cue from each accepted semantic change. Keep the current cue with its screen, row, or side as appropriate. A later accepted change to the same target replaces it on that frame. Simultaneous 1P and 2P moves each get their own cue. A rejected or clamped input and a packet that changes nothing produce no cue. Keep long presses and fast reversals responsive. Clear cues when their owner is discarded or its target changes, and record title stamps at the destination after a successful transition.
- Draw effects in the existing painters. Use `Fight::layout` and the renderer’s native text tile projection (`TextEdits`) for the native HUD elements. Keep clipping and layering in the same path as the elements they animate. The lifebar trail stays inside the existing bar, and shake moves only the designated bar and name. Compose at native resolution before host scaling and the CRT look. Preserve existing overlay ordering, stage transforms, wipes, and special scene composition. Settings and Pause accents draw over the presented frame. Local Pause can re-render the same fight state when Gauge Art changes, while its menu cues leave the fight frame number intact and stay out of the stored backdrop.
- Shared renderers also serve online play and replays. Fight effects follow those instances’ simulation state. Shared menu accents use accepted local and replicated changes, with no new network payload. Online Pause remains a live neutral-input overlay, and the separate online After Match vote screen keeps its existing vote presentation and both-player readiness rules. A transition from that flow into the shared Stage Select can use its normal destination title stamp.

## Fight HUD

Frames below are tuning targets from the preview at the game’s normal 60 Hz cadence. Frame zero is the first rendered frame of the real event. Retain the existing display rules when an element is absent.

| Element | Treatment and trigger |
| --- | --- |
| Lifebars | On a hit that reduces displayed life, apply the new red fill immediately. Leave a short amber trail in the newly lost span and a subtle 2-pixel marker at the new edge. Give an ordinary hit about 2 frames of hold and 8 of decay, with a marker up to 5 frames. Give a heavy hit about 4 frames of hold and 12 of decay, with a marker up to 8. Use existing impact classification where the defender’s hit cue is available and the ordinary envelope for a visible hit without a heavy cue. Keep the recent tail bounded inside raw displayed damage during rapid hits. Heal, refill, round reset, and a zero-life transition must clear the ordinary cue. |
| Combo counter | When the visible count grows, stamp the digit about 2 pixels into place over 5 frames and add an edge spark for about 8. On the existing combo-finish event, add a warm highlight and a short stroke for roughly 13 frames for 5+ hits. Preserve the counter’s normal reveal, lifetime, and Hold Combo. |
| Era gauges | On an earned readiness threshold, snap the stock bevel on, sweep the affected fill, and flash its label cream. On a real spend, discharge to the new amount over 12 frames and tick the label amber. Snap for roughly 8 frames and keep the label accent under 10. Use `GaugeEffects`’ actual row transitions, including spend distinctions for Drive and V-Trigger. Preserve the existing steady full flash and ready pulse on settled values. Apply the treatment to Ruled, Blocks, and Plate with each artwork’s own shapes and clipping, including mirrored, secondary, shared, and independent ally gauges. |
| Round timer | Turn the displayed digits amber from `010` through `001`. At each real tick, flash and recoil only the changed digits by one pixel and settle in 5 frames. Read the visible timer and respect the match’s count cadence and freezes. TIME UP uses the native flow and lettering. |
| K.O. | On the first native knockout frame, apply a short amber flash on the emptied lifebar and stamp `K.O.` in ROM lettering. Shake the loser’s name and lifebar together with a ±3, ±2, ±1 pixel horizontal settle over about 12 frames. Flash for up to 14 frames and clear the stamp within about 18 or sooner if a native result or wipe needs that space. Keep the existing stage shake independent. A timeout follows TIME UP. If the native flow resolves a double K.O., use a single stamp and flash the emptied bars without assigning a loser to shake. |
| Round-win markers | When a native marker first fills for the awarded round, give that marker a warm flash and a brief bevel glint lasting about 8 frames. Detect the award and visible slot together so an existing marker returning after a wipe stays settled. Use the same accent on a round awarded by timeout. |
| Fight call-outs | Introduce each word with a 2-to-1 pixel inward slide over 6 frames and a short warm entrance. SUPER and PERFECT PARRY get the brighter flash and small dash, up to 12 frames. Keep ALPHA COUNTER’s existing gauge flash. Preserve normal slot order, call-out lifetime, separate move-name strips, existing flashes, and announcer behavior. Draw within existing reserved regions when several events land together. |

Fight integration begins at [`Fight::step_with_pad_override` and `Fight::layout`](../../crates/sf-engine/src/front/fight.rs), using [`FightRead` and `ComboCounter`](../../crates/sf-engine/src/fight_reader.rs), [`GaugeEffects`](../../crates/sf-engine/src/front/fight/gauge_effects.rs), and [`CalloutBoard`](../../crates/sf-video/src/callouts.rs). Use the native health spans and slots as the source of geometry for the two main bars. Test lifebar motion when gauges and call-outs are absent as well as when they overlap. In Training, cue a visible loss when it occurs and leave existing refill, no-round, and timer rules in force.

In Dramatic Battle, put the trail on the visible bar that actually lost life, using the dedicated bar renderer for an independent second ally. A single ally falling while the other can still fight follows ordinary lifebar rules. The K.O. stamp is reserved for the round-ending native K.O. Flash an emptied bar if it is visible and shake it together with its label if the renderer still shows that pair. Keep auxiliary bar visibility and the round outcome under the existing Dramatic Battle rules.

## Menus

Use the native pixels and stable layout of each screen. Simple offsets return to their exact resting positions. While a cue is active, the current selection and value remain actionable.

| Element | Treatment and trigger |
| --- | --- |
| List cursor | Arrow kick and row flash together on a real selection change. The arrow kicks about 2 pixels then settles, and the selected row flashes warm. Finish in 5–6 frames. Ordinary list movements in the approved screens share the treatment. |
| Confirm and Back | A title stamp as the destination draws for the first time, shifting its existing title by 2 then 1 pixels over about 5 frames. Reuse the framed header or Main Menu logo painter. A Confirm that changes a value uses value feedback, and a stage confirmation starts the existing blink. |
| 1P and 2P cursors | Each moved cursor gets a short corner flash and a 2-to-1 pixel corner settle. Keep the labels in their native positions for the new selection, with no kick or tint. When both players occupy one tile, maintain each label and half of the bracket throughout the settle. |
| Character portraits | On each affected side, apply a quick palette snap and 2-to-1 pixel slide, clipped to the portrait. Preserve names, frame, cursor labels, and the other portrait. Settle in about 5 frames. |
| Style card and lock-in | A clipped Scanline latch as the card opens and a brief latch accent on lock. Keep the three rows and selections readable. Once the last player locks, Stage Select arrives with its title stamp on the existing transition frame. |
| Stage Select | Combine tile snap, preview slide, preview scan, and caption flash for about 5–8 frames on a changed stage. Draw using `StageScreen`’s current preview and tile rectangles, including expanded stage banks and Random. A repeat input replaces the previous cue immediately. |
| Settings and Pause values | On a real displayed value change, combine value flash, 2-to-1 pixel nudge, and a direction tick in about 7 frames. Use the accepted input’s direction through wrapping and reversal. On a toggle, reset, or Confirm without a direction, use the flash when the value changes. Sample fast left/right cycles on Gauge Art and ensure the underlying gauge art still changes through the existing settings path. |
| Local After Match and rematch | Stamp the option rows as the box first opens, using the same short 2-to-1 pixel settle. Apply the normal arrow kick and row flash on row moves. Rematch immediately opens Stage Select with its title stamp, retaining fighters and styles, the previous stage, and the game’s assigned picker. The loser picks after a decisive match. Preserve the existing draw fallback. Confirming the stage uses the normal cabinet blink and full native fight intro. |

The natural entry points are [`GameSession::screen_input`, `pause_input`, and `after_match_input`](../../crates/sf-engine/src/front/session.rs) and the screen modules for [menus](../../crates/sf-engine/src/front/menu.rs), [Character Select](../../crates/sf-engine/src/front/select.rs), [Style cards](../../crates/sf-engine/src/front/style_card.rs), [Stage Select](../../crates/sf-engine/src/front/stage_select.rs), [Settings](../../crates/sf-engine/src/front/settings.rs), [Pause](../../crates/sf-engine/src/front/pause.rs), and [After Match](../../crates/sf-engine/src/front/after_match.rs). Render functions use their own coordinates. The general `Transition::CursorMoved` can also describe a Style edit or Settings tab change, so the screen must identify what actually moved.

## Build order and checks

1. **Lifebar trail and list cursor.** Wire an ordinary and a heavy hit through `Fight` and the existing compositor, and rapid navigation through Main Menu and Multi Player. Establish the two clocks and verify both in the shipped game’s normal entry points. Check that damage and menu selection update on the original frame, that rapid inputs replace motion, and that the settled elements match the baseline. Prove rollback restores a transient hit frame and that the Faithful 1987 frame still matches.
2. **Combo, era gauges, and fight call-outs.** Wire grow, finish, readiness, spend, and priority presentation through their existing owners. Check a heavy hit that also grows a combo, gains a stock, or adds a call-out. Cover three Gauge Art styles, partial spends, regeneration, timed drain, and both sides. Verify replay and rollback of overlapping cues with the same inputs.
3. **Countdown and round finish.** Add per-digit feedback, K.O., loser shake, and the round marker. Check both losing sides, final-count ticks, actual timeout, double K.O. when produced by native flow, held and frozen states, and that an earlier lit marker stays quiet in the next round.
4. **Titles and setup screens.** Add title stamps, Character Select cues, Style latch, and Stage Select cues. Drive real confirm and Back inputs, simultaneous moves, sharing and leaving a tile, lock and unlock, rapid reversals, and stage confirmation. Compare the native transition frame and cabinet blink before and after. Check replicated changes through shared selection screens.
5. **Settings, Pause, and After Match.** Add value motion and local result stamps. Check a local Pause backdrop stays frozen, two directions through wrapped values, online Pause advancing the fight, and local rematch retaining the previous stage and correct picker. Replay the full approved route in-game and compare it against the reel.

Extend the existing front-end, gauge presentation, Training, replay, and netplay checks where they can observe a new regression. Use focused frame or region comparisons for timing and clipping with fixed inputs. Use native playthroughs for readability and feel, including Ryu (SF2 ST) versus Ken (SF5 CE) on Joe’s stage. The builder can tune exact pen choices, amplitudes, and durations within the approved look after side-by-side checks. The preview’s browser speed and motion controls are review aids. For the game, use the existing Screen Shake preference for the K.O. HUD shake as well as its current stage shake. The K.O. flash and stamp still show with Screen Shake off.
