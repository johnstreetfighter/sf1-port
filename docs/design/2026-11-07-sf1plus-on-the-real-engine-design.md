# SF1+ on the real engine

## 1. Intent

The ten 1987 bosses (Retsu, Geki, Joe, Mike, Lee, Gen, Birdie, Eagle, Adon and Sagat) become real, playable fighters in real fights, with the poses the art pipeline made for them, and Arcade opens to all twelve. Today a boss in a player's hands only fires his 1987 CPU attacks from the buttons, and none of the new poses appear in play. This is project 3 of the overhaul, and it comes before netplay so that the maintainer and a friend can play the whole roster online (the maintainer, 2026-11-07: order 3, then netplay, then 4, 5, 6).

There are two levels of SF1+, and both are built before netplay starts (the maintainer):

- **The classic level** plays like 1987: every fighter has Ryu's kind of kit under 1987 rules. No combos, no cancels, no throws.
- **The modern level** adds true combos, cancels from normals into specials, and techable throws.

## 2. Goals, non-goals, anti-goals

### Goals
- **Every fighter gets Ryu's level of kit** (the maintainer): standing, crouching and jumping normals on all six buttons, high and low blocking, and his specials on command motions, drawn with his own poses. This kit is the same at both levels (my call, the maintainer agreed: the modern rules are switches, not a second kit).
- **The specials are the old spec's list** (the maintainer):

  | Fighter | Specials |
  | --- | --- |
  | Retsu | Kiai Shot |
  | Geki | Shuriken, Teleport Strike |
  | Joe | Rolling Sobat, Dynamite Straight |
  | Mike | Bison Rush, Iron Upper |
  | Lee | Zesshouohken, Double Kick |
  | Gen | Hyakurenko, Gekiro (no stance switch) |
  | Birdie | Bull Head, Bull Horn |
  | Eagle | Manchester Black, St. Andrew's Green |
  | Adon | Jaguar Kick, Rising Jaguar |
  | Sagat | high and low Tiger Shot, Tiger Knee |
  | Ryu, Ken | Hadoken, Shoryuken, Tatsumaki Senpukyaku (unchanged) |

- **Two levels as presets** (the maintainer):
  - **Classic Competitive**, the classic level: the preset now called *SF1++ Competitive* is renamed. 1987 rules: a fighter can't be hit during his hit reaction, no cancels, no throws (the maintainer: "No throws for classic").
  - **Definitive Overhaul**, the modern level: true combos, normal-to-special cancels, and throws that can be teched. Ryu and Ken get the modern rules too (my call).
  - **Faithful 1987** is untouched: Ryu and Ken only, the 1987 CPU, the 1987 rules, still MAME-exact.
  - **Pure Footsies** plays the classic rules (my call) with Balanced roster off (the maintainer, grilling Q3): its job stays responsive controls and 1987-strict commands, with all twelve fighters.
  - The modern rules are separate switches (combos, cancels, throws), like the existing core toggles, so a custom preset can mix them (my call).
- **A Balanced roster switch** (the maintainer agreed to my proposal). Every move carries its "1987 character" values: a boss's existing attacks keep the timing, reach and damage his ROM data gives them, and his new moves are tuned to match that character. The switch applies a tuning layer of numbers only (damage, startup, recovery, hitbox sizes and positions and the like, never animations or which moves exist) so the twelve come out roughly even. It's off in Classic Competitive and on in Definitive Overhaul, and any preset can flip it. The layer is per fighter where that's enough (say, all of Sagat's damage down 15%) and per move only where it isn't.
- **The CPU learns the new kit and plays fair** (the maintainer: "fair like a human"). Outside Faithful 1987, CPU opponents (Arcade, and Training's CPU) use the full kit at whichever level is on. They react with a human's delay, never read the opponent's inputs, and the difficulty setting decides how well they play.
- **Arcade opens all twelve** outside Faithful 1987. **The stand-in is Ryu** (the maintainer, grilling Q6, Q8, Q11; this replaces the front-end design's rule that the country's other fighter stands in): when the player is one of a country's pair, Ryu takes the player's own slot and is fought on that slot's stage. A Lee player meets Ryu and Gen in China; a Sagat player meets Adon and then Ryu as the final battle, on the Thailand stage. **Ryu brings his own music** (the maintainer): whenever Ryu is the CPU opponent in Arcade (every stand-in fight and Sagat's final battle), his theme plays instead of the stage's: an arrangement of his SF2 theme for the 1987 sound chip, kept in the local music pack like every Capcom melody (stages-and-music design, section 4). If the pack lacks it, the stage's own 1987 theme plays and the log says so.
- **A boss's Arcade clear** goes straight to the 1987 staff roll and name entry, skipping the ending art (the maintainer: endings are a separate project; my call for the placeholder). Project 5 slots each fighter's ending in ahead of the staff roll later.
- **Every new move is in the move list**, so MOVES, Show and Try work for all twelve in Training (my call).
- **Tuning is Claude's** (the maintainer: "I'll let you tune it"). The maintainer plays the whole roster when the project is done and asks for one-off adjustments. Tuning draws on the research in `docs/research/sf1plus-reference-numbers.md` (being written alongside this design; tuning waits for it): frame data and hitboxes from these fighters' later games, fighting-game design theory, and how fair CPUs are built.

### Non-goals
- Boss endings, Dramatic Battle and World Tour (project 5).
- Era styles and gauges on the real fighters (project 4). The era HUD's combo count does come alive here, because the modern level gives combo-able hitstun.
- Netplay (the next project). This project only keeps the fight deterministic so netplay can be built on it.
- Bonus challengers (project 6).
- Supers or meters of any kind: SF1+ has none.
- New poses beyond the art pack's catalogue. If a move needs a pose the catalogue lacks, that is a finding to bring to the maintainer, not a silent art job.

### Anti-goals (a failure even if it works)
- **Two engines.** Under the overhaul, Ryu stops being the 1987 Ryu, or a boss runs on different movement, blocking or command code from Ryu's (the maintainer chose approach A, section 4).
- **The front end writing fight state into the machine's RAM** between frames (ADR-0004). Everything goes through inputs or overhaul-only gates in translated code.
- **Faithful 1987 changing** by a byte or a pixel, or failing its MAME checks.
- **A fight that doesn't replay the same way** from the same inputs: anything random, time-based or held outside the machine's snapshot. Netplay is next and depends on this.
- **A CPU that reads inputs** or reacts faster than a person could. Its strength must come from choices and delays, not from seeing the future.
- **A boss's move that shows the wrong fighter**, or Ryu's or Ken's pose, or a pose from outside the approved catalogue.
- **A fighter whose hitboxes don't match what's drawn**: an attack that hits where nothing is drawn, or a body that can't be hit where it is drawn. This binds the boxes this project writes; ROM moves kept with their ROM tiles keep their ROM boxes (5.1).
- **The game failing without the art pack.** It must still start and play (see 6.4).

## 3. What exists today (found while designing)

- **A boss in a player's hands today** (`fighter.rs` `human_boss_input`, `roster.rs` `boss_action`). The roster writes the fighter's ROM move table to the player structure's `+0x12` every frame, through an overhaul gate. Ryu and Ken go through the 1987 player handler. A boss goes through `boss_action`, which maps the six buttons onto six of his CPU attack streams (a fixed table per boss), plus a few specials (Geki's shuriken and teleport, Sagat's Tiger Shot and Tiger Knee, Eagle's barrage and leap). A stream that reaches the CPU follow-up (`0x45630`) just ends. There are no command motions.
  - **Block:** no boss can. Blocking needs move 4 or 8, which only Ryu's handler starts.
  - **Crouch:** only Retsu, Geki and Mike. Slot 5 is empty for six bosses. Joe's slot 5 hits the untranslated branch `0x44506`, which is why Joe mirrors are barred (`MIRROR_NOT_CLEAN`).
  - **Jump:** everyone except Sagat, whose jump slots are empty.
  - **Throws:** nobody, Ryu and Ken included. The 1987 game has no throws.
  - **Hit reactions:** a boss uses the CPU's hit and knock-out tables (`reaction_tables`).
- **Ryu and Ken are one player program on two data tables.** The move table at `+0x12` is what makes a fighter Ryu or Ken. That is the seam approach A uses.
- **The poses** (`art/recipes/*.json`, the art pack, ADR-0007). All twelve fighters' catalogues are approved and load into the extended sprite bank (tile slots from `0x3800`; 12,555 slots today) in overhaul fights. No move stream points at them yet, so no new pose appears in play. Each pose is one rectangle of at most 24 tile entries in one palette, with an anchor. The pipeline made no hitboxes: the 1987 frame record (0x20 bytes) carries the boxes, and this project writes them. New projectile and effect art (the Kiai Shot's burst and the like) also belongs here.
- **The SF1+ model** (`crates/sf-engine/src/sf1plus.rs`) is a standalone practice engine with placeholder numbers, distance checks for collision and procedural tiles. It is not on the real fight's path, and approach A does not build on it. Its tests stay as they are or are retired, at the builder's call.
- **Hitstun.** Every 1987 fighter is invulnerable for his whole hit reaction, so there are no true combos. The era HUD's combo count already waits for combo-able hitstun. (True combos are built: see 9.1.)
- **Arcade** greys the ten bosses on the select by scope decision only (`front/select.rs`). The ladder report shows the engine copes with every boss as the player (Joe excepted, on `0x44506`). The stand-in rule is designed but not built.
- **Presets** (`config.rs` `RulesetPreset`): Faithful 1987, SF1++ Competitive, Pure Footsies 1987+ and Definitive Overhaul, plus Custom. The SF1++ Competitive name appears in the settings file (`sf1_plus_plus_competitive`), the Settings screen and the docs.
- **The CPU.** Arcade's opponents run Capcom's 1987 CPU routines with each boss's CPU attack set. Training's CPU is the same.

## 4. Approaches (the maintainer picked A)

**A. Bosses become 1987 player fighters (chosen).** In the 1987 engine, Ryu and Ken are one player program running two data tables. Each boss gets a Ryu-format table in an extended data bank, the way the poses went into an extended sprite bank, and the translated 1987 player code runs all twelve: the same walking, jumping, blocking and command reading. The modern rules are overhaul-only gates in that code (combo-able hitstun, cancel windows, throws). The CPU feeds pad inputs into the same code, as a delayed, human-like player. *Trade:* one engine for everyone, true to 1987, deterministic for netplay, and inside ADR-0004; but the move data has to be written in the 1987 formats (frame records with hitboxes), and some format limits may need extending.

**B. A new fighter engine for the overhaul presets.** Grow `sf1plus.rs` into a modern data-driven engine and swap it in for the 1987 player code when an overhaul preset is on. *Trade:* the easiest to author and tune, with no format limits; but two engines, Ryu no longer the 1987 Ryu under the overhaul, and against the maintainer's rule that overhaul mechanics act on the real 1987 fighters (2026-11-02).

**Dropped:** a hybrid (the 1987 engine for movement, a new one for attacks). It has B's two-engine problem plus a seam between them.

Why A (the maintainer, 2026-11-07, on my recommendation): it keeps the game one game, the format work is a known, bounded cost, and the art pipeline already proved the extend-the-1987-format route.

## 5. Decided shape of the work

### 5.1 The kit, in the 1987 formats
- Each boss gets a player move table in Ryu's format, held in an extended data bank that the translated player handler reads through an overhaul-only gate. Faithful 1987 never sees the bank. Ryu's and Ken's ROM tables stay as they are.
- Each move is 1987 move streams and frame records: tiles from the boss's catalogue, the pose's anchor turned into the record's offsets, and hit, hurt and push boxes written for the pose.
- A boss's existing 1987 attacks keep their ROM streams and boxes where they fit a player move; that is where his "1987 character" comes from. New moves (crouching and jumping normals, blocks, specials the CPU set lacks) are authored.
- **Boxes and the drawing** (my call): a ROM move kept with its ROM tiles keeps its ROM boxes as they are, Ryu's and Ken's included. Those boxes are the 1987 character, quirks and all. The rule that boxes match what's drawn binds every box this project writes, and any ROM move shown with a new pose gets new boxes.
- **The ROM box audit** (the maintainer, 2026-11-07, on my recommendation): every ROM move players use, Ryu's and Ken's included, has its attack boxes measured against the drawn limb and its body boxes against the drawn body, with reach and speed compared against the research's reference numbers. The outliers go in a report to the maintainer before any box is adjusted. Box adjustments (size and position) live **only in the Balanced roster layer** (the maintainer): with it off, as in Classic Competitive, every ROM box stays exactly as 1987 had it.
- Command motions go through the 1987 command trackers, the same ones Ryu's specials use, so Pure Footsies' strictness and the lenient-commands toggle apply to everyone.
- Mike's and Eagle's kick buttons start punch and stick moves, as the art pipeline decided.
- The boss-only driving path (`boss_action`, `human_boss_input`) is deleted once every boss runs on his table (6.4; builder's call on timing). Joe's `0x44506` problem goes away with it; his mirror is no longer barred.

### 5.2 The modern rules (Definitive Overhaul)
- **True combos:** a gate makes the hit reaction hittable, with hitstun and blockstun from move data instead of whole-reaction invulnerability.
- **Cancels:** a gate opens a cancel window on normals that the move data marks as cancelable, into specials.
- **Throws:** close-range throws with a tech window, using the throw, thrown and get-up poses. None at the classic level.
- Each is its own switch, in the core-toggle style, and the presets bundle them.
- Ryu and Ken get the same rules.

### 5.3 Balance (the maintainer, on my recommendation)
- One set of "1987 character" values per move, plus the Balanced roster layer (section 2). The layer is data, applied through the same gates, never a second copy of a move.
- The layer may also override a move's box sizes and positions, after the ROM box audit (5.1).
- A move's "1987 character" values are its true ROM numbers where the ROM has the move.
- The two older rebalance switches, `balanced_damage_and_chip` and `symmetric_shotos_and_blockable_dp`, fold into Balanced roster, so the game never rebalances twice. Their behaviour (special damage, multi-hit knockdown scaling, stage chip, symmetric Ryu and Ken, a blockable single-hit Shoryuken) becomes part of the Balanced roster layer.

### 5.4 The CPU
- A new CPU for the overhaul presets that plays through the pad: it decides from what it can see on screen, after a human-like reaction delay, and presses buttons like a player. It never reads the opponent's inputs.
- The Gameplay tab's difficulty (the match settings, the maintainer, grilling Q4) sets its reaction delay, how often it makes the right choice, and how well it executes. The 1987 cabinet's DIP difficulty stays the faithful cabinet's only.
- Faithful 1987 keeps Capcom's CPU. Under overhaul presets, Arcade's opponents and Training's CPU use the new one.
- Determinism: any randomness is seeded from the fight itself, so the same inputs give the same fight.
- **As built (SP21, #264; SP22, #265; `crates/sf-engine/src/fair_cpu.rs`).** The machine holds the CPU and sets Player 2's stick and buttons before each frame, as a pad would (ADR-0004: an input, not a RAM write); the match configuration's `p2_fair_cpu` gives it the pad, and Faithful 1987 never does. Training against the CPU under an overhaul preset is therefore a two-player Training fight, like the dummy's, and its opponent can be any fighter the 1987 player code runs: Ryu, Ken, and all ten bosses once their tables are in the extended data bank.
  - *What it sees* each frame: both fighters' places, facing, the move and state each is drawn in, whether an attack box is live and how far it reaches, the loose hurtboxes, the health bars, and the opponent's nearest projectile. Never an input byte, latch or command tracker.
  - *When*: it decides from the frame its reaction delay ago. Easy 27, Normal 19, Hard 15, Very Hard 12 frames (inside the research's bands, 5.4); a motion special given in answer costs 6, 4, 3 or 2 frames more.
  - *How*: utility scoring in neutral (walk in or out, wait, guard in range, each normal that reaches, its specials, a jump-in, and, when the modern switches are on, link strings under `true_combos`, normal-to-special cancels under `cancels`, and close-range throws under `throws`); things it sees start are answered with the level's chance (35, 60, 80, 92%): a guard of the right height, a timed anti-air, a jump over or trade with a projectile, a punish of a whiff (including a cancelable normal into a special under `cancels` or a close jab link under `true_combos`), or a crouching guard with a delayed throw tech against an opponent walking into throw range under `throws`. It takes the best option 45, 65, 80 or 90% of the time, otherwise one of the next best. Specials come from the move registry, and what each is for from its slot record in the fighter's table (SP4's special slots): a melee special on the Shoryuken's rising tracker (`0x19`, or guarded crouching in the air: the Shoryuken, Joe's Dynamite Straight, Mike's Iron Upper, Gen's Gekiro, Adon's Rising Jaguar, Sagat's Tiger Knee) is the anti-air, one neither melee nor guarded crouching in the air (the Hadoken, the Shuriken, the Kiai Shot, Sagat's high and low Tiger Shots) a projectile, any other (the Tatsumaki, the Teleport Strike, the Rolling Sobat, the Bison Rush, Lee's Zesshouohken and Double Kick, Gen's Hyakurenko, Birdie's Bull Head and Bull Horn, Eagle's Manchester Black and St. Andrew's Green, Adon's Jaguar Kick) carries the fighter in. So a boss's specials reach the CPU when his table and registry entries do, with no change to the CPU. A special's motion is clean 70, 85, 93 or 97% of the time, less under pressure, and a slip drops the motion's middle step or presses early.
  - *What it sees as an attack* is an attack move drawn (`0x0d..=0x2f` excluding `GET_UP_MOVE` `0x28`), never the state byte's attack bits alone: the 1987 jump code sets those as soon as a button is pressed for a jump attack still to come (review finding, 2026-11-08).
  - *Under the modern rules* (SP17..SP19, SP22): it attacks only into an opening it can use. An attack still coming is guarded, so a guard locked in blockstun holds through the string, and a guarded move is never punished as a whiff. A hit reaction (or a `"get-up"`, `GET_UP_MOVE`) is no opening either: it is seen a reaction delay late, so most of its hitstun has gone. Each modern tactic is gated by its own switch in `fair_cpu::Context` (`true_combos`, `cancels`, `throws`, section 9.4), so under `ClassicCompetitive` (or with its switch off) the CPU never scores or plans it.
  - *What it learns*: each normal's reach and its projectile's range from its own attack boxes and shots, longer or shorter than its starting guess (Ryu's reaches; a projectile thought to cross the screen until seen), so a short blast like the Kiai Shot is soon kept for close range.
  - *Its state* (what it saw, its plan, its random generator, what it has learned) is in the machine's snapshot; the generator is seeded from the fight the first time it sees it.

### 5.5 Arcade
- All twelve selectable outside Faithful 1987, with the stand-in rule on the opponent-load path as an overhaul-only gate.
- A boss's clear goes to the staff roll and name entry, with no ending art.

### 5.6 Mirror palettes (the maintainer, grilling Q5, Q10; scope settled 2026-11-08)
- With Joe's crash gone, any fighter can face himself in Local. Every fighter, Ryu and Ken included, gets a second palette for the second side of a same-fighter fight, picked by hand from his own colours (as SF2 did alternate colours) and approved by the maintainer on the review page (`sf-art palettes`). No generation, so no credits. The palettes are committed under `art/palettes/`.
- **Body only** (the maintainer, 2026-11-08): projectiles, bursts, effects and the hit sparks keep the ROM's colours. A palette file is one body palette: the ROM sprite palette it replaces, a free sprite palette (14) and 15 pens.
- **Where it applies**: Local, Training against a dummy and Training against a CPU, under every overhaul preset; never under Faithful 1987, even with FULL CAST switched on in the Roster tab. The gate is `Bus::mirror_palette_pass` at the end of each machine frame, an overhaul-only gate on the translated path (ADR-0004): it writes the palette into palette RAM and points the second side's 24-entry region at it, so Faithful 1987 and every other fight are byte for byte as the ROM draws them.
- Colours are the fighter's own, except where the maintainer chose a colour the fighter's ROM palette lacks (Retsu's saffron, Eagle's navy).

### 5.7 Training and the move list
- The move registry gains every fighter's moves, so MOVES lists them and Show and Try work for all twelve.

### 5.8 Classic Competitive (the maintainer, on my recommendation)
- *SF1++ Competitive* becomes *Classic Competitive* on screen and in the docs. A settings file that names the old preset still loads, as Classic Competitive.
- It keeps the control switches on (positive-edge attacks, input buffering, lenient commands, interruptible walks, anytime jump attacks, standard high and low blocking) and has Balanced roster, combos, cancels and throws off.

### 5.9 New effect art (the maintainer, on my recommendation)
- Effects the catalogue lacks (the Kiai Shot's burst and any other new projectile or effect) are made the art pipeline's way: each piece goes on the review page for the maintainer's approval, and the maintainer is asked the credit cost of each generation round before it's spent.
- The Kiai Shot is a short-range blast that fades out in front of Retsu, not a full-screen projectile (the maintainer, 2026-11-08). Its six frames (two at full strength, four fading), each one 32×32 effect sprite in his own palette, came from one approved round of 30 generations and were approved on the review page on 2026-11-08; they are in his recipe as `kiai_blast_1..6`. A round waiting for approval is reviewed from `art/pending/` (`sf-art review --recipes`), since an unapproved pose in a recipe keeps the fighter's whole set out of the game.

## 6. Constraints

### 6.1 The 1987 formats
- The frame record's box formats and table sizes bound what a move can do. Where a move needs more than a limit allows, the builder extends the format through an overhaul-only gate, as ADR-0007 did for tiles, and records why.
- Poses: one rectangle of at most 24 tile entries, one palette (ADR-0007).

### 6.2 ADR-0004
- No RAM writes from outside the engine. Fighter choice reaches the engine through the roster settings; every behaviour difference is an input or an overhaul-only gate on the translated path.

### 6.3 Determinism
- Everything that decides a fight is in the machine's state and its snapshot, so rollback, replays and RESET POSITIONS agree with a fight played straight through.

### 6.4 Without the art pack (the maintainer, on my recommendation)
- The game must start and play without the pack. Without it, outside Faithful 1987, the ten bosses can't be picked: their tiles are greyed on every select, with the log saying why. Ryu and Ken play as normal.
- So the boss-only driving path (`boss_action`, `human_boss_input`) is deleted, not kept as a fallback, and Joe's `0x44506` problem and his mirror ban go with it.
- Netplay (the next project) must check at the handshake that both players have the same pack, since a fighter's kit depends on it. Recorded here so it isn't lost. The maintainer's friend needs a copy of the art pack, which lives in the maintainer's private repo.

### 6.5 Tests
- The fight is tested through its inputs and what it presents, as the rest of the engine is: a move's startup, active and recovery frames and its boxes against its data; command motions; blocking high and low; the modern switches each on and off; the balance layer; the stand-in rule; the CPU's determinism and its reaction delay; Faithful 1987 still MAME-exact.
- The boss ladder report runs every fighter through Arcade.

## 6b. Terms (grilling, written to `CONTEXT.md`)
- `SF1` is the 1987 game's own Ryu and Ken, as Faithful 1987 plays them; `SF1+` is the one era style all twelve play under the overhaul presets until project 4 adds later eras (the maintainer, grilling Q1).
- **Classic rules** and **modern rules** name the two rule sets; the presets carry them, and "level" is not a term (the maintainer, grilling Q2). **Balanced roster**, **1987 character values** and the new **stand-in** are in the glossary too.

## 9. As built

### 9.1 True combos (SP17, #261)

The `true_combos` switch (SP1) has its behaviour: `crates/sf-engine/src/combo/` and the gates marked SP17 in `fighter.rs`, all reading the switch, so with it off the fight is the 1987 fight byte for byte. Tests: `tests/true_combos.rs` (through the game session) and `tests/reaction_boxes.rs`.

- **A ground reaction becomes hittable.** A hit that puts a living defender in a ground reaction (his hit table entry's state is standing `0x8a` or crouching `0x8c`) starts a combo-able reaction: its frames lose the invulnerable flag and take real hurtboxes, and it lasts the attacking move's hitstun, cut short or held on its first frame to fit. The countdown runs once a fight frame before the input handlers, so the defender acts on the frame it ends whichever side the dispatcher runs first (1987's own frame order would have made links jitter by a frame).
- **No juggles, combos on the ground (my call, for the maintainer).** Airborne reactions stay 1987's, invulnerable throughout: a hit on a jumping fighter, and the ROM's knock-backs. The ROM's damage entries send a standing fighter flying back (reactions `0x33..0x35`) on the heavies, the medium kicks, the sweep, the stronger jump attacks and every special, so on a standing opponent those end a combo; the light normals and the medium punches (`0x30..0x32`) leave him on his feet. Against a crouching opponent every hit is a ground reaction (`0x3a..0x3c`). Knock-outs and chip (a guarded projectile or Tatsumaki, whose 1987 reaction is a stagger) stay 1987's too. Mike's crouching reactions are airborne in his ROM table, so a crouching Mike can't be comboed.
- **Blockstun.** A guarded melee hit locks a player's guard for the move's blockstun; it can switch between high and low, nothing else, and a button pressed then waits in the latch (and the recovery buffer) for the first free frame. The 1987 CPU's guards are its own timed streams and take none. 1987 has no blockstun at all (a blocker can attack on any frame of his guard), so this is new under the modern rules.
- **One hit per move.** Once a move connects (hit or guarded) its later active frames stay dormant until a new move starts. In 1987 the reaction's invulnerability did this; without it a move whose active frames span several records would hit on each. So the Tatsumaki and an unbalanced Shoryuken hit once, as in 1987. The Tatsumaki also keeps its 1987 rule that it doesn't hit a fighter already reacting, so nothing combos into it.
- **Where the numbers live (move data).** `combo::move_stun`: a row per move id for each fighter's player moves. Ryu's and Ken's (one table: their 1987 frame data is the same for every normal), Retsu's, Geki's, Joe's, Mike's, Lee's, Gen's, Birdie's, Eagle's, Adon's and Sagat's are tuned; any other fighter's moves, and the 1987 CPU's attack streams, take the research's SF6 medians by strength class (light 15 / 10, medium 22 / 17, heavy 26 / 20, sweep 26 / 18; jump attacks by strength; specials and projectiles 26) until his own ticket adds his rows. Each boss ticket (SP5..SP12) should add its fighter's rows the same way. Geki's moves all recover slowly (19 to 31 ticks after their first active tick), so none is plus on hit and his lights and sweep sit at the caps: his normals are pokes, not combo starters. At the closest spacing his normals fall short of Ken's guard pose (they whiff it rather than being blocked), seen while testing, not changed here. Joe's 1987 jab, straight and low kick recover quickly (5 to 14 ticks), so his jab (+5) links into itself from point-blank (further out his partial jab's stagger pushes the opponent out of reach, as Retsu's); his low kick into a straight, crouching kick and sweep sit at the caps (SP5, #252). His standing guard is his ROM block, a duck whose 1987 hurtbox is the lower two thirds of its grid: Ryu's standing punches and medium kick pass over it, so it takes no blockstun from them (the ROM's boxes, kept as 5.1 says; listed for the maintainer). Mike's 1987 jab (+5) recovers in 7 ticks and links into itself from point-blank; his 1987 lunging hook, uppercut and crouching hook recover in 19 to 23 ticks and his new standing hook and crouching sweep in 17 to 19, so those sit at the caps (SP6, #253). - **How the rows are tuned.** Each normal gets an advantage by class: light +5 on hit and -1 guarded, medium +3 / -2, heavy +2 / -4, sweep 0 / -10 (SF6's medians, research 3.2), so stun = the ticks the move runs after its first active tick + the advantage + 2 (the frames from a hit to the next move's first frame: the stream's end is processed on the tick after its last frame, and the input handler starts the next move a frame later; measured in the tests). Capped at the research's largest stun per class (light 18 / 14, medium 26 / 20, heavy 28 / 22), so a move with a long 1987 recovery stays minus rather than getting an SF6-sized stun. Jump attacks and specials take the class numbers (a jump-in's advantage depends on how deep it lands; nothing links after a special). The tables with each move's recovery and advantage are in `combo/mod.rs`.
- **What links (Ryu, Ken).** Tried from close range, every pair of the standing and crouching normals (a scratch test, not kept): against a standing opponent the standing jab, the standing medium punch, the crouching jab and the crouching short each link into a standing jab, the only normal fast enough (startup 4); against a crouching opponent nothing links (his low normals start in 7 or more against at most +5, and a standing jab passes over a crouch). The pushback of the reactions' first frames ends the strings: a third jab is out of reach. Cancels (SP18) are where longer combos will come from.
- **One hit per move removes a 1987 double hit.** The 1987 heavy kick can hit twice two frames apart, its second hit record meeting the defender in the frames before his reaction pose loads; under true combos it hits once (Training's hardest pressure on the CPU no longer reaches the health floor because of it; `tests/training_health_floor.rs`). Retsu's jab links into itself only from close (his jab's partial hit staggers with reaction `0x32`, whose pushback takes the opponent out of reach from further out; a clean hit knocks back); his crouching punch and kick have 24- and 14-tick recoveries after the strike, so they sit at the caps and are minus.
- **Hurtboxes for the reactions.** Every ground hit-reaction frame of all twelve fighters' 1987 hit tables (231 frames) has boxes measured from its drawing (`combo/derive.rs`, written into `combo/reaction_boxes.rs`; `tests/reaction_boxes.rs` checks the table is current). A frame that draws the fighter's stance keeps the stance's 1987 boxes; any other keeps the reference posture's box sizes (the 1987 torso box) and places them on the drawn body (the median drawn column, the drawn rows) with the head box at the reference's distance below the drawing's top. Every box passes the ROM box audit's thresholds: at worst 13 points more of the drawing outside than the stance (flag 30), 9 px past the drawing (flag 24), 4 px of critical box outside (flag 16).
- **Combo damage scaling.** SF6's general schedule (research 3.5): the first two hits whole, then 80, 70, 60 ... down to 10 %, rounded, never below one point. Needed because a 1987 hit is coarse (a light 1 or 2 of 48, a heavy 8 to 10, a special 20); it rarely bites, since the knock-backs end most combos after two or three hits, but it keeps a long string on a crouching guard in check.
- **Fight reader.** Hitstun reads as before (a reaction move while busy), and is now the move's hitstun; blockstun comes from the machine's combo state (`FightReader::read_with_combos`), so REVERSAL can now be earned out of blockstun.
- **Determinism.** The state (`combo::ComboState`: hitstun, blockstun, the combo's hit count, each move's connected flag) rides in `CoreToggleState`, in every machine snapshot; a combo replays the same from a restored snapshot (tested). It is kept per player structure, not per object slot, because frame flags `0x10` / `0x20` trade the two fighters' object regions mid-move (Ryu's sweep, heavy kick and Tatsumaki do).
- **Fight reader.** Under true combos a landed hit is into hitstun when the engine counted it as its combo's second hit or later; under the classic rules the reader keeps its 1987 rule (in a hit reaction the frame before).

### 9.2 Cancels (SP18, #262)

The `cancels` switch (SP1) has its behaviour: `crates/sf-engine/src/combo/cancel.rs` and the gates marked SP18 in `fighter.rs`, all reading the switch, so with it off the fight is the 1987 fight byte for byte. Tests: `tests/cancels.rs` (through the game session).

- **The window opens on contact, hit or guarded (my call, for the maintainer).** When a normal the move data marks cancelable connects (the judge's hit function clears its active bit), the attacker's cancel window opens for 12 frames, counted from the frame after the contact. A whiff opens none: the research's conservative convention, SF2's (specials cancel a normal on hit or block). The window closes early when the move ends, another move starts or the fighter is hit. 1987 has no hitstop, so the window is real time; 12 frames keeps a cancel a reaction to the contact, and every cancelable normal of Ryu's and Ken's ends within 12 frames of its contact, so for them it is the rest of the move. Retsu's and Geki's longer recoveries are where it closes first (Geki's crouching jab runs on 10 frames past it).
- **A special's command cancels the normal.** In the window, a button whose special's command is done at the direction held (the 1987 rule the dispatch fires specials by, strict or lenient) starts the special at once, through the 1987 direction dispatch, so the special, its strength and the trackers it clears are those of the same input on a free frame. Any other button does nothing: no chains into normals. 1987 already runs the command trackers every frame before the busy gate, so the motion can be made during the normal; only the button was lost (the handler's tail drops it).
- **A press just before the contact counts.** A special pressed in the frames before the window opens is held by the recovery input buffer (4 frames in the presets, recording whether the special's command was done when the button was pressed) and taken on the window's first frame, as modern games buffer a cancel; a normal press followed by a motion does not cancel. Without the buffer switch only presses inside the window count.
- **Which normals cancel (my call, for the maintainer).** One rule by the 1987 input handler's move ids, which mean the same posture and button on every player table (Ryu's and Ken's ROM tables and the bosses' bank tables): the standing light punch and light kick, the standing medium punch, every crouching light punch, the crouching medium punches and the crouching light and medium kicks. Never a heavy, the sweep, the standing medium kick, a jump attack or a special. This is SF2's classic set; the research (3.6) notes which normals cancel is a balance lever (ST's Old Sagat lost his standing-kick cancels to stop projectile spam). The 1987 standing medium kick is slow, recovers long and knocks a standing opponent back through the air, a heavy in all but name, so it stays out. Ryu, Ken, Retsu, Geki, Joe, Mike, Lee, Gen, Birdie, Eagle, Adon and Sagat all take the rule; a boss ticket that wants a different mark for one of his moves adds his own rows to `combo/cancel.rs`, alongside his stun rows in `combo/mod.rs`. The bosses still on the boss-only controls don't run the 1987 handler, so they can't cancel until their tickets.
- **Cancel combos.** A cancel is a new move that connects on its own, so under true combos it counts on into the combo running: its hit count, the damage scaling and the era HUD's counter. Measured (tests): Ryu's and Ken's crouching short into the Hadoken combos on a standing or crouching opponent (the fireball lands 11 frames after the cancel, inside the short's 18 frames of hitstun); into the Shoryuken it combos on a crouching opponent from the closest spacing, while on a standing one the short's reaction pushback leaves him out of the uppercut's reach. Retsu's jab into the Kiai Shot combos only when cancelled at once (measured: cancelled 2 frames after the contact, the blast lands in the jab's 16 frames of hitstun; 6 frames after, it doesn't). Nothing combos into the Tatsumaki (SP17 kept its 1987 rule that it doesn't hit a reacting fighter).
- **Determinism.** The window (`combo::cancel::CancelState`: frames left, the move that opened it, a count of cancels) rides in `CoreToggleState`, in every machine snapshot, per player structure like the combo state; a cancel replays the same from a restored snapshot (tested).
- **Found while building and fixed here:** SP17's strength classes (`combo::class_of`) and stun rows (`SHOTO`, `RETSU`, `GEKI`) called moves `0x12` and `0x13` a medium and a heavy punch and `0x1d` a heavy punch, when `0x11..=0x13` are the three crouching light punches (down, down-forward, down-back, `docs/reference/crouch-attacks.md`) and `0x1c..=0x1e` are the crouching medium and heavy punches. Both `combo::class_of` and the stun rows now match the crouching punch move ids.

### 9.3 Throws and throw techs (SP19, #263)

The `throws` switch (SP1) has its behaviour: `crates/sf-engine/src/combo/throw.rs`, the throw and get-up streams built into `crates/sf-engine/src/data_bank.rs`, and the gates marked SP19 in `fighter.rs` and `fight_reader.rs`, all reading the switch, so with it off the fight is the 1987 fight byte for byte. Tests: `tests/throws.rs` (through the game session across all twelve fighters).

- **Input and range.** Pressing forward or back and heavy punch or heavy kick on a free standing frame while within `RANGE` (`48` pixels centre-to-centre) starts the throw (`THROW_MOVE`, `0x27`) on the thrower and the being-thrown sequence (`THROWN_MOVE`, `0x3e`) on the opponent, pulling the victim to `GRAB_GAP` (`32` pixels) in front of the thrower. Outside `48` pixels the input falls through to the normal 1987 dispatch (`0x10` standing heavy punch or `0x16` standing heavy kick).
- **Who can be thrown.** Only a living, grounded opponent who is neither in hitstun (`+9 & 8 != 0` or `combo::hitstun > 0`), nor in blockstun (`combo::blockstun > 0`), nor airborne (`+9 & 3 == 1`), nor already in a throw or `"get-up"` (`0x27`, `0x28`, `0x3e`), nor in his post-wakeup throw invulnerability (`wakeup_invuln > 0`).
- **Throw techs.** When a throw connects, a 7-frame tech window (`TECH_WINDOW = 7`, SF2T/SF6 scale) opens on the victim while the first grabbed pose holds. If the victim inputs forward or back and heavy punch or heavy kick while his tech window is open, both fighters tech: both return to standing (`move 1`), push apart by `TECH_PUSH` (`28` pixels each), play the block sound, and take no damage.
- **Landing side (playtest correction, 2026-11-10).** A throw follows its authored motion. Ryu, Ken, Geki, Joe, Lee and Eagle carry the victim across to the opposite original side: shoulder, circle, hip, over-back and rear-shoulder throws. Retsu, Mike, Gen, Birdie, Adon and Sagat keep the victim in front for a forward throw: wrist flip, shove, palm strike, head slam and clinch. Back input still reverses these front throws. This replaces the earlier generic rule that every forward throw leaves the victim on the same side; the maintainer explicitly corrected Ryu and Ken's shoulder throws during playtesting.
- **Arc, slam, get-up, and wakeup throw invulnerability.** Once the 7-frame tech window closes (`ROUTINE_THROW_ARC`), a crossing throw turns the participants and carries the victim continuously across the thrower during the arc, without an instantaneous side relocation. Ordinary standing separation does not interrupt that crossing. Front throws keep their outward travel. At `ROUTINE_THROW_SLAM` the slam deals `DAMAGE` (`6` HP, raw unscaled throw damage, resetting any active combo count) and spawns the knockdown dust effect (`0x1861c`); if the slam knocks the victim out (`health == 0`), he stays down (`+8 = 8`) and ends at `ROUTINE_THROW_GET_UP`. Otherwise `ROUTINE_THROW_GET_UP` switches his move id to `GET_UP_MOVE` (`0x28`) for his catalogue's `"get-up"` poses (`FighterRead::getting_up` is true and `FighterRead::attacking()` is false, so `REVERSAL` fires on the first wakeup frame), and `end_move` grants `WAKEUP_INVULN` (`13` frames) of post-wakeup throw invulnerability. Lying and get-up push extents follow each pose's ground anchor so the native vertical clamp does not lift a short pose off the mat.
- **All twelve fighters.** `DataBank::build` builds each fighter's `"throw"`, `"thrown"`, and `"get-up"` streams from his approved catalogue (`ThrowsEntry` per `TournamentFighter`), including Ryu and Ken (whose normal move tables stay in ROM while their throw and thrown headers live in a shared bank region, with a ROM-tile fallback when no art pack is attached).
- **Authored pose direction.** Ryu/Ken's `throw_turn` and `throw_heave`, and Lee's `throw_turn`, `throw_heave` and `throw_recover`, already turn away from the original opponent in their artwork. Their catalogue records mark that intrinsic turn so the drawing and anchors mirror opposite the logical heading. The grab, restored stance, other throw poses and ROM-only fallback retain ordinary mirroring. The thrower therefore visibly turns after the grab and still faces the landed opponent in recovery.
- **Determinism.** `combo::throw::ThrowState` (`tech_window`, `wakeup_invuln`, `crosses`) rides in `CoreToggleState`, in every machine snapshot, per player structure like `ComboState` and `CancelState`. `ThrowState.v2` records the authored crossing decision rather than only the input direction; the canonical tag changes with that meaning.

### 9.4 The CPU uses the modern rules (SP22, #265)

`fair_cpu::Context` carries `true_combos`, `cancels`, and `throws` from `CoreToggles`, and `FairCpu` gates each modern tactic on its switch (`crates/sf-engine/src/fair_cpu.rs`, `crates/sf-engine/tests/fair_cpu.rs`):

- **Link combos (`Choice::Combo`, gated by `true_combos`).** Scored in neutral within close range (`dist <= 56`) and on close whiff punishes (`gap <= 32`): the CPU walks into point-blank jab range (`dist <= 38`) and links standing jabs (`button(0)` pulsed on a 2-frame cadence), subject to `Level::execution()` (dropping to a single jab on an execution slip).
- **Normal-to-special cancels (`Choice::Cancel { crouch, button, role }`, gated by `cancels`).** Scored in neutral and on whiff punishes when a cancelable normal (`crouching LK`, `standing LP`, `standing MP`) reaches and the fighter has a special in `Kit` (`Projectile` when no shot is on screen, else `Advance` or `AntiAir`): the CPU presses the normal, inputs the special's command motion from `moves::move_list`, and pulses the special's button across the 12-frame cancel window (subject to `Level::execution()` and pressure).
- **Throws and delayed throw techs (`Choice::Throw` and `PlanKind::Guard`, gated by `throws`).** In neutral within `dist <= 72` (scored higher inside `dist <= 48` and against a guarding opponent), `Choice::Throw` walks into close contact (`dist <= 38`) and presses `Forward` or `Back` with `HP` or `HK`. Defensively, when `react` sees an opponent walking forward (`move 2`) into throw engagement range (`46 + reaction + 8`), the CPU enters crouching guard (`Plan::guard(true, 16)`) with `Level::guard()` probability, and `PlanKind::Guard` in `next_pad` pulses a delayed `Back + HP` tech input (`held >= 3 && held % 3 == 0` when `view.dist() <= 46` and no attack threat is seen), teching throws inside the 7-frame `TECH_WINDOW` while obeying the difficulty's reaction delay (`Easy` 27, `Normal` 19, `Hard` 15, `VeryHard` 12 frames).
- **Get-up perception.** `Body::attacking` excludes `GET_UP_MOVE` (`0x28`) and `Body::reacting` includes it, so the CPU treats a rising opponent as busy/invulnerable rather than attacking.

### 9.7 The Balanced roster tuning (SP20, #268)

The tuning lives in `crates/sf-engine/src/balanced_roster.rs` as plain tables, each row carrying its reason; the engine reads them only with `balanced_roster` on, so Classic Competitive and Faithful 1987 play every ROM value and box exactly. Three hook points: `Bus::load_frame` (`apply_balanced_frame`: boxes, the body's front edge, duration and flags of a frame by its ROM address, before the facing flip), `Bus::start_move_at` (`apply_balanced_move_start`: the two move headers below), and the hit and stun paths (`cpu_damage`, `shoto_entry_damage`, `second_hit_stun`).

- **Joe's standing guard (the maintainer, SP5).** His block pose `0x0e370` (and the move 4 header on his ROM table `0x1734c` and bank table) carried a hurtbox over only his lower two thirds, so Ryu's standing punches and medium kick passed over a guarding Joe with no blockstun. - **The SP3 audit (`docs/reference/rom-box-audit.md`).** Attack boxes over nothing (the shotos' sweep frames `0x01178` / `0x03256`, moved onto the drawn leg with the reach kept); boxes past the drawing or long reach (Retsu's high kick, Birdie's low kick, Joe's second strike, Sagat's high kick), trimmed to the drawn limb (Retsu's jab stays as the ROM has it: any trim leaves it short of a standing guard at the spacing his jab-into-Kiai-Shot cancel needs); point hurtboxes mid-move (Geki's crouching claw, Mike's straight, Lee's jumping attacks, Birdie's hammer and headbutt), given the pose's hurtbox; critical boxes outside the hurtbox and hurtboxes that miss the drawing (the shotos' jump back, Tatsumaki and jump HK; Geki's claw; Mike's hook recovery, whose half-height was 8 for 44; Lee's landing; Birdie's jab; Gen's and Adon's bodies), brought over the drawn body; the two dormant CPU attack frames (Gen `0x103d6`, Sagat `0x13ec6`) made live.
- **Startup (`docs/research/sf1plus-reference-numbers.md`).** Sagat's CPU Tiger Shot spawned on tick 9, under the research's 12–16 tick projectile range; its wind-up `0x144b4` holds 10 ticks so it spawns on tick 12, as the Hadoken.
- **Advantage.** Joe's low kick into a straight was tuned from its first strike; a spaced hit on the second strike alone (`> 0x0e76c`) was +6 on hit and +1 guarded. The second strike takes `stun(19, 14)` (+3 / -2).
- **Damage.** The research puts a 48-point bar's lights at 3–5, mediums at 6–8, heavies at 8–11 and specials at 9–12. Everything else on a CPU hit function keeps the specials' cap of 12; the special values already tested (Hadoken 9, Shoryuken 10, Tatsumaki 9, Tiger Shot 10, chip 1) are unchanged.
