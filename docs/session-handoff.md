- **Stronger Fair CPU (spec #192; SC1 #193, SC2 #194 and SC3 #195 built 2026-11-18 and 19; pushed through bedb5e6 and closed 2026-11-19):** **The switch** is `MatchConfig::stronger_cpu`, off everywhere; the `--stronger-cpu` launch flag of `sf-game` turns it on through `GameSession::with_stronger_cpu`, and no menu does. It is not sent to a netplay peer, so the session keeps it off online until SC14 (#206), and Dramatic Battle's CPUs ignore it until SC23 (#215). The replay file does not record it, so a fight played with it on stays in the session's replay list but is not written to disk; whether to add it to the replay format is an open question for the maintainer. **With it on** the Fair CPU judges one of eight situations plus a corner flag from the frame its reaction delay shows it (`fair_cpu/game.rs`, `Situation`, `Judged`), and Ryu in the SF6 era style follows one game plan in close neutral: walk to crouching medium kick range, press it, then a projectile after a hit it sees, a walk-in throw after a guard, or giving ground and guarding after a whiff. A game plan is a table of steps, each naming the step that follows a hit, a guard, a whiff, a jump or nothing; the CPU's state holds only where it is in one. A threat it answers, or being hit, ends the plan. While a plan is followed the Utility AI adds no command (for SF2 to SF6 styles it otherwise takes the pad on any frame the Fair CPU's pad is empty, which is what SC8 #200 removes). Pairs with no game plan play as before. Tests: `tests/stronger_cpu.rs` (five fight tests on the bare machine) and unit tests in `fair_cpu/game.rs`; the 13 tests of `tests/fair_cpu.rs` pass unchanged with the switch off. The canonical tags `FairCpu.v1` and `PowerOnGameplay.v2` became `v2` and `v3`, so state hashes differ from older builds even with the switch off; play does not.
