## Problem Statement

Players returning to the 1987 **original game**—or playing its faithful **port**—confront severe mechanical, content, and tooling limitations that have frustrated the community for nearly four decades. Even on the six-button **reference version**, software emulation of the deluxe cabinet's pneumatic pads delays every normal and special attack until button release, saturates holds after 255 frames, drops inputs made during attack recovery, resets special-move command trackers after 18 frames or immediately upon leaving the final direction, and locks out special moves and crouches during 8 of every 10 walk frames. In combat, special moves strip 41.7% health (withPlayer 1 Ryu's Dragon Punch being both frame-1 invincible and completely unblockable), stage-indexed chip damage strips up to 33.3% health per blocked special before dropping to zero against Thailand bosses, standing guard fails against low attacks, crouching guard is triggered by jumping attacks that hit it anyway, unblocked fireballs trigger a 90-frame slow-motion crawl, and Sagat's CPU AI ignores cabinet difficulty settings. Furthermore, ten of the twelve tournament fighters are locked as incomplete CPU-only bosses, Ken cannot be selected directly in one-player mode, and official re-releases omit training tools, arranged audio options, single-player challenge modes, and online rollback netplay.

At the same time, replacing the **original game** with a loose remake or sacrificing the verified **port** would lose the historical artifact this project preserves. The player needs both an uncompromised, frame-accurate **port** of the **reference version** and a comprehensive **overhaul**—styled to look and sound like it came from the same 1987 arcade hardware—where every mechanical fix, fighter, historical **era style**, **era gauge**, and mode is selectable and toggleable.

## Solution

Deliver a unified PC product that pairs the verified faithful **port** with a toggleable **overhaul** built on a two-tier hybrid architecture. Once the faithful **port** achieves the **complete arcade experience** against the **comparison baseline**, the **overhaul** becomes the default **ruleset preset**, while the faithful 1987 **port** remains a one-click **ruleset preset** with 100% preserved **gameplay fidelity**, **visual fidelity**, and **audio fidelity**.

Tier 1 introduces surgical **core toggles** directly on the recovered 1987 gameplay routines (positive-edge button registration, recovery input buffering, lenient command trackers, interruptible walk cycles, post-apex jump attacks, standard high/low/jump blocking, balanced special and chip damage, symmetrical Ryu/Ken frame data with blockable Dragon Punches, removal of the 90-frame fireball hit slowdown, true-second round timers, and difficulty scaling for Sagat and Adon). Tier 2 introduces a data-driven **overhaul** layer that keeps the `384×224` pixel resolution and 15-color-plus-transparent sprite art rules while lifting internal hardware table ceilings via an Extended Scene Compositor. It makes all twelve fighters playable across every **era style** and major arcade revision they canonically appeared in (`SF1`, `SF1+`, `SF2 WW` through `ST`, `SFZ1` through `Z3`, `SF3 NG` through `3S`, `SF4` through `USF4`, `SF5` through `CE`, and `SF6`), pairs each **era style** with its **era gauge** by default (while allowing match toggles to decouple movesets from gauges), supplies missing 1987-style animations and optional **era costumes** via an offline AI sprite-generation and palette-quantization pipeline, encodes new voice callouts and sound effects into authentic 1987 8 kHz ADPCM and FM synthesis formats alongside a Red Book CD audio Jukebox, and surrounds combat with a 12-fighter Arcade Mode, Versus, an *Alpha 3*-inspired 1987 World Tour, 2v1 Dramatic Battle, a modern Training Mode with hitbox/hurtbox overlays and Replay Takeover, multiple control schemes, and deterministic rollback netplay.

## User Stories

1. As a preservation-minded player, I want to select the `Faithful 1987` **ruleset preset** at any time, so that I can play the exact **reference version** with zero alterations to its timing, pixels, sound, or quirks.
2. As a player launching the completed game, I want the `Definitive Overhaul` **ruleset preset** active by default once the faithful **port** is finished, so that the game immediately plays and feels responsive and complete out of the box.
3. As a competitive retro player, I want an `Classic Competitive` **ruleset preset** with optional sub-format ban lists, so that I can play all twelve fighters under raw 1987 engine rules matching established community tournament formats.
4. As a footsies purist, I want a `Pure Footsies (1987+)` **ruleset preset** that enables completed movesets and responsive **core toggles** while disabling all **era gauges**, so that matches focus purely on spacing, pokes, and fundamentals.
5. As a tinkerer, I want a `Custom` **ruleset preset** where I can flip any individual **core toggle** or **overhaul** rule and save my configuration, so that I can tailor the exact blend of 1987 and modern mechanics I prefer.
6. As a player, I want a **core toggle** for positive-edge attack registration, so that normal and special attacks fire on the frame I press a button rather than waiting until I release it.
7. As a player using positive-edge attacks, I want an optional negative-edge sub-toggle for special moves, so that special moves can trigger on either button press or button release as in classic 1990s entries.
8. As a player, I want the 255-frame button-hold saturation lock removed when input improvements are enabled, so that holding a button for several seconds never eats a subsequent release.
9. As a player, I want a **core toggle** for a recovery input buffer, so that attack and special inputs pressed a few frames before hitstun, blockstun, or attack recovery ends execute on the first actionable frame instead of being discarded.
10. As a player, I want a **core toggle** for lenient special-move command trackers, so that I have a wider frame window, diagonal shortcut tolerance, and directional release leniency after completing a motion.
11. As a player, I want a **core toggle** that allows special moves, blocks, crouches, and jumps to interrupt the gated frames of the 1987 walk cycle, so that walking forward or backward never eats my *Hadoken*, *Tatsumaki*, crouch, or jump inputs.
12. As a player, I want the instant 16-pixel first-frame backward walk shift suppressed when starting a back-held normal attack in **overhaul** modes, so that point-blank back light kicks do not whiff unexpectedly.
13. As a player, I want a **core toggle** that allows jump attacks to fire during ascent, at the apex, or during descent (and optionally shortens the 9-frame pre-jump crouch), so that deep jump-in attacks work intuitively.
14. As a defender, I want a **core toggle** for standard high/low/jump blocking rules, so that holding Back reliably blocks standing and jumping attacks without walking into low attacks, and holding Down-Back blocks low attacks without getting hit through crouching guard by jumping attacks.
15. As a defender, I want proximity blocking to work cleanly and the 16-frame hurtbox disappearance bug ("Omniblock") on Lee and Adon fixed when standard blocking is enabled, so that defense is consistent across all fighters.
16. As a player, I want a **core toggle** that rebalances special-move damage and stage chip damage (and prevents multi-hit instant-kill collision bugs), so that rounds do not end in two fireballs or a single point-blank Dragon Punch.
17. As a player, I want a **core toggle** to turn chip-damage KOs ("chip death") on or off, so that we can choose whether blocked special moves can finish off a low-health defender.
18. As a player, I want a **core toggle** that normalizes baseline frame data between Ryu and Ken and makes *Shoryuken* blockable with vulnerable landing recovery, so that Player 1 Ryu is not an unbeatable tournament ban.
19. As a player, I want a **core toggle** that disables the 90-frame slow-motion crawl on non-lethal projectile hits while keeping slow-motion on final round-winning KOs, so that fireball exchanges run at a smooth 60 FPS.
20. As a player, I want a **core toggle** that ticks the round timer once per real second (60 frames) and resolves tied three-round matches with a Sudden Death / Draw round instead of an automatic CPU win, so that time management and tiebreakers feel fair.
21. As a single-player contender, I want a **core toggle** that adds human-like reaction delay to the CPU's input-reading branch, makes Retsu's unblockable attack blockable, and gives Sagat and Adon distinct Easy/Normal/Hard/Very Hard AI difficulty tables, so that difficulty settings actually govern the final bosses.
22. As a player, I want all twelve fighters from the 1987 tournament (Ryu, Ken, Retsu, Geki, Joe, Mike, Lee, Gen, Birdie, Eagle, Adon, and Sagat) selectable across Arcade, Versus, World Tour, Dramatic Battle, Training, and Online modes, so that none of the cast is locked to the CPU.
23. As a player, I want every fighter to have both an `SF1` **era style** (raw 1987 behavior) and a completed `SF1+` **era style** with full six-button standing, crouching, and jumping normals, standard high/low blocks, ground throws, and command specials, so that formerly incomplete bosses like Sagat, Mike, and Geki play like complete 1987 characters.
24. As a fan of the wider series, I want any fighter who appeared in a later entry to have a selectable **era style** for every major arcade revision they canonically appeared in (`SF2 WW`, `CE`, `HF`, `Super`, `ST`; `SFZ1`, `Z2`, `Z2A`, `Z3`; `SF3 NG`, `2I`, `3S`; `SF4`, `SSF4`, `AE2012`, `USF4`; `SF5`, `AE`, `CE`; and `SF6`), so that I can play any historical incarnation of that character in 1987 pixel art.
25. As a player on the Character Select screen, I want a two-step `Era Family -> Revision (& Loadout)` selector after picking my fighter, so that I can quickly navigate characters with up to eighteen revisions and select pre-match loadouts (such as ISMs, Super Arts I/II/III, Ultras I/II/Double, or V-Skills/V-Triggers I/II).
26. As a player selecting an **era style**, I want that style to equip both its historical moveset and its signature **era gauge** and system mechanics by default (such as `SF2 ST` Super, `SFZ` Alpha Counter and Custom Combos, `SF3` Parry and Super Arts, `SF4` Focus Attack and FADC, `SF5` V-System, or `SF6` Drive Gauge), so that each revision genuinely feels like its source game.
27. As a match host, I want a match toggle to decouple movesets from **era gauges** (*Era Default*, *No Gauges / Pure Footsies*, or *Unified Gauge*), so that players can use later-era movesets without comeback meters or share a single meter rule across both fighters.
28. As a player in a cross-era match (such as `SF1` Ryu vs. `SF6` Ken), I want the attacker's **era style** to govern hitstun and comboability, the defender's **era style** to govern blocking and defensive mechanics, and health to be normalized across styles (with an optional toggle for unscaled 1987 42% special damage), so that cross-era combos and defenses interact predictably.
29. As a player, I want all twelve fighters to have techable ground throws and anti-throw-loop corner pushback by default in `SF1+` and later styles—with toggles for untechable classic *SF2* throws and corner throw loops—so that close-range offense has a throw option without degenerating into binary corner throw loops.
30. As a competitive player, I want individual safety toggles to disable raw Drive Rush neutral inertia, health-gated comeback scaling, and screen-freeze input lockouts, so that players can tune out the most criticized mechanics of modern entries and never have inputs eaten during Super or Drive flashes.
31. As a player, I want every **era style** to default to the fighter's authentic 1987 outfit design while offering optional 1987-pixel-art **era costumes** (such as bearded `SF6` Ryu or `SFZ`/`SF5` Birdie) alongside unlocked color palettes, so that the game stays visually anchored in 1987 while celebrating each fighter's history.
32. As a player, I want all newly generated sprite animations, **era costumes**, character endings, and HUD elements to obey the `384×224` resolution and 15-color-plus-transparent 1987 sprite palette rules via an Extended Scene Compositor in **overhaul** modes, so that new content is visually seamless with the original 1987 art and never causes sprite-limit flickering during 3-fighter battles.
33. As an audio enthusiast, I want all new voice callouts and sound effects for the ten bosses and later **era styles** encoded in authentic 1987 4-bit 8 kHz MSM5205 ADPCM and YM2151 FM formats by default, so that new moves sound indistinguishable from the original arcade sound board.
34. As a music fan, I want an in-game Audio Jukebox and Sound Test that lets me switch per stage or character between native 1987 YM2151+MSM5205 synthesis and external Red Book CD arranged tracks (such as the 1988 PC Engine CD *Fighting Street* arranged soundtrack), so that I can enjoy either arcade or arranged music.
35. As a player, I want to choose per player between Classic 6-Button controls, Simulated Pneumatic 2-Pad controls (using button hold duration or analog gamepad triggers to vary strength), and Modern Controls (4-button layout with a toggleable startup/damage penalty on 1-button neutral specials and supers), so that historical, competitive, and casual control preferences are all supported.
36. As a single-player fan, I want a full 12-character Arcade Mode where I can pick any fighter and **era style**, choose any of the four starting countries (Japan, USA, China, England) before Thailand, play positive-edge-friendly bonus stages, and view unique 1987-style pixel-art rival dialogues and character endings for all twelve fighters.
37. As a single-player fan, I want an *Alpha 3*-inspired "1987 World Tour" mode where I take any of the twelve canonical fighters across a five-country branching mission map featuring special rule modifiers, Survival, Time Attack, and style mastery trials—with RPG stat/perk progression available as an optional toggle and zero content locked out of Arcade, Versus, Training, or Online modes.
38. As a co-op or exhibition player, I want a 2v1 Dramatic Battle mode (such as Ryu and Ken teaming up against Boss Sagat) where all three fighters are clamped inside the `384×224` camera view, the two-player team defaults to a shared *Alpha*-style lifebar (toggleable to independent lifebars), and friendly fire defaults to off (toggleable to on).
39. As a CPU match player or spectator, I want the CPU to use the faithful 1987 table AI when playing in `SF1` style and a Style-Aware Utility AI when playing in `SF1+` through `SF6` styles (including in CPU vs. CPU mode), so that computer opponents intelligently use special cancels, Parries, FADCs, V-Triggers, and Drive mechanics.
40. As a learner, I want a Training Mode with a real-time Hitbox/Hurtbox Viewer that displays the 1987 engine's outer loose box (`depth 2`), inner tight/critical box (`depth 1`), active attack and projectile boxes, pushboxes, and invulnerability flags, so that I can see exact collision boundaries.
41. As a learner, I want a 60-cell color-coded Visual Frame Meter and a live 1987 Command-Tracker & Walk-Gate Inspector in Training Mode, so that I can see startup, active, recovery, cancel windows, frame advantage, and why a special move or walk input succeeded or failed on any frame.
42. As a learner, I want pre-built Training Mode scenario drills (anti-air, whiff punish, throw tech, combo trials) and **Replay Takeover** (allowing me to pause any saved match replay and immediately take control of either fighter for 10 seconds), so that I can lab real match situations without manually programming the dummy.
43. As an online player, I want deterministic snapshot-based rollback netplay that synchronizes the host's match configuration (**ruleset preset**, active toggles, and PRNG seed) at handshake while letting each player pick their fighter, **era style**, **era costume**, control scheme, and local-only Jukebox BGM.
44. As an online player outside major regions or avoiding unstable connections, I want an open custom room browser and matchmaking with live ping indicators, a wired vs. Wi-Fi indicator, a permanent **Wired-Only Auto-Filter** toggle, and zero geographic region locks, so that I can match quickly with any low-ping opponent worldwide.
45. As the developer, I want non-invasive diagnostic overlays (the Hitbox/Hurtbox and Command-Tracker inspector) usable in the development launcher during Phase 1 faithful recovery, while keeping all gameplay-altering **overhaul** phases sequenced after the faithful arcade-flow batch completes, so that **gameplay fidelity** verification is never compromised.

## Implementation Decisions

- **Two-Tier Hybrid Architecture (ADR-0002):**
  - **Tier 1 (`CoreToggles` in the native 1987 engine):** Implement surgical 1987 mechanical fixes directly at the translated 68000 routine boundaries on the memory-mapped `Bus`. When all **core toggles** are disabled (`Faithful 1987` **ruleset preset**), execution is 100% byte-identical to the **reference version** on the **comparison baseline**.
  - **Tier 2 (Data-Driven Overhaul Layer):** Implement multi-era movesets, **era gauges**, cross-era interaction rules, 3-fighter Dramatic Battles, the Style-Aware Utility AI, 1987 World Tour, Training Mode overlays, and Rollback Netplay in a high-level data-driven layer above the core engine, video renderer, and audio synthesizer.
- **Core Toggles Contract (Tier 1):**
  - `positive_edge_attacks`: Latches punch/kick strength on button press instead of button release (`release + 1`), removes the 255-frame hold saturation lock, and provides an optional negative-edge trigger for special moves.
  - `recovery_input_buffer`: Buffers attack and special inputs within a configurable window (default 4 frames) prior to the end of attack, hitstun, or blockstun recovery instead of clearing latches in the input handler tail.
  - `lenient_command_trackers`: Expands the 18-frame (`0x12`) command tracker window, keeps completed Stage 3 (`done = 0xff`) valid for a configurable buffer window after releasing the final direction, and accepts diagonal shortcuts.
  - `interruptible_walk_cycle`: Allows special moves, blocks, crouches, and jumps to interrupt gated walk frames (`0x91`) immediately, and suppresses the first-frame 16-pixel back-walk shift when dispatching a back-held attack.
  - `anytime_jump_attacks`: Permits jump attacks during ascent, apex, or descent rather than exclusively at the jump apex routine, and optionally trims the 9-frame pre-jump crouch.
  - `standard_high_low_block`: Standing guard blocks standing and jumping attacks without walking backward into low attacks; crouching guard blocks low attacks and fixes the bug where jumping attacks trigger crouching guard (`move 8`) but only check standing guard (`move 4`); enables proximity guard before active frames; fixes the 16-frame hurtbox disappearance bug on Lee and Adon.
  - `balanced_damage_and_chip`: Scales special-move damage down from 41.7% (20/48 HP), prevents multi-hit/scuttle instant-kill collisions, normalizes stage chip damage across all stages (including Thailand), and exposes a `chip_death` toggle.
  - `symmetric_shotos_and_blockable_dp`: Makes *Shoryuken* blockable with vulnerable landing recovery and normalizes baseline normal/special frame data between Ryu and Ken.
  - `disable_fireball_slowdown`: Bypasses the 90-frame slow-motion event (`Scene Event 9`) on non-KO projectile hits while retaining slow-motion on final round-winning KOs.
  - `real_second_timer_and_draws`: Ticks the round timer every 60 frames instead of every 19 scheduler passes, and awards a Sudden Death / Draw round on a tied match instead of an automatic CPU win.
  - `fair_cpu_and_sagat_scaling`: Adds configurable reaction delay to the CPU's frame-1 input-reading branch, makes Retsu's unblockable move blockable, and populates distinct Easy/Normal/Hard/Very Hard AI tables for Sagat and Adon.
- **Era Styles, Sub-Revisions, and Cross-Era Combat Rules (Tier 2):**
  - Every fighter receives `SF1` and `SF1+`, plus a distinct top-level **era style** for every major arcade revision in which they canonically appeared (`SF2 WW`, `CE`, `HF`, `Super`, `ST`; `SFZ1`, `Z2`, `Z2A`, `Z3`; `SF3 NG`, `2I`, `3S`; `SF4`, `SSF4`, `AE2012`, `USF4`; `SF5`, `AE`, `CE`; `SF6`).
  - Character Select uses a two-step `Era Family -> Revision (& Loadout)` selector, where Loadout covers ISMs, Super Arts I/II/III, Ultras I/II/Double, or V-Skills/V-Triggers I/II.
  - By default, selecting an **era style** equips both its moveset and its signature **era gauge**; match toggles allow decoupling gauges (*Era Default*, *No Gauges / Pure Footsies*, or *Unified Gauge*) and toggling off raw Drive Rush inertia, meterless corner throw loops, comeback scaling, and screen-freeze input lockouts (inputs are continuously buffered through all screen freezes).
  - In cross-era matches, the attacker's **era style** governs hitstun, juggle, and combo rules; the defender's **era style** and active toggles govern blocking and defensive mechanics; health is normalized to a shared internal scale (mapped to the 11 HUD cells) with a toggle for unscaled 1987 42% special damage.
  - All twelve fighters receive techable ground throws in `SF1+` and later styles, with wakeup corner throw pushback enabled by default to prevent meterless throw loops, and toggles for untechable *SF2* throws and corner throw loops.
- **Extended Scene Compositor, AI Asset Pipeline, and Audio Encoding:**
  - `Faithful 1987` renders exclusively through the unmodified 1987 hardware renderer (64 sprite entries, 16 sprite sub-palettes, 2bpp 1,024-tile text layer).
  - **Overhaul** modes render at the exact same `384×224` pixel resolution and 15-color-plus-transparent per-sprite rule through an Extended Scene Compositor that removes the 64-sprite-entry and 16-palette ceilings, adds a pixel-art HUD layer for **era gauges**, and composites a post-frame overlay layer for the Hitbox Viewer and 60-cell Frame Meter.
  - Missing animations, **era costumes**, and character endings are generated offline via an AI image-generation tool conditioned on the decoded 1987 sprites, quantized to 15-color-plus-transparent sub-palettes, and packed into `16×16` tiles in external asset packs mounted only in **overhaul** modes.
  - New voice callouts and move sound effects are encoded into authentic 1987 4-bit 8 kHz MSM5205 ADPCM and YM2151 FM formats by default, alongside an Audio Jukebox supporting external Red Book CD arranged tracks.
- **Control Schemes, Modes, CPU AI, and Rollback Netplay:**
  - Per-player control schemes support Classic 6-Button (default), Simulated Pneumatic 2-Pad (via hold duration or analog triggers), and Modern Controls (4-button layout with a toggleable startup/damage penalty on 1-button neutral specials and supers).
  - 1987 World Tour lets players use any of the twelve canonical fighters across a five-country mission map with optional RPG stat/perk progression; all fighters, **era styles**, **era costumes**, and palettes are unlocked from the start in Arcade, Versus, Training, and Online modes.
  - 2v1 Dramatic Battle clamps all three fighters inside the `384×224` camera window, defaults the two-player team to a shared lifebar (toggleable to independent), and defaults friendly fire to off (toggleable to on).
  - CPU opponents use the translated 1987 table AI when playing in `SF1` style and a Style-Aware Utility AI when playing in `SF1+` through `SF6` styles.
  - Rollback netplay operates on deterministic frame state snapshots, synchronizes the host's `MatchConfig` (**ruleset preset**, active toggles, PRNG seed) at handshake while allowing per-player fighter/style/costume/control choices and local-only Jukebox BGM, and provides an open room browser with ping indicators, a **Wired-Only Auto-Filter** toggle, and no geographic region locks.
- **Phased Sequencing:**
  - Phase 1 completes the faithful 1987 arcade-flow batch first (allowing only non-invasive diagnostic overlays in the development launcher earlier if helpful for debugging).
  - Phase 2 delivers Tier 1 **core toggles**, raw `SF1` 12-character selection (*SF1++*), and the Training Mode overlay (Hitbox Viewer, 60-cell Frame Meter, Replay Takeover).
  - Phase 3 delivers the Extended Scene Compositor, offline AI asset pipeline, complete `SF1+` 12-fighter movesets, 12-character Arcade Mode with endings, and Audio Jukebox.
  - Phase 4 delivers the `SFII` through `SF6` **era styles**, sub-revisions, **era gauges**, **era costumes**, and Style-Aware Utility AI.
  - Phase 5 delivers 1987 World Tour, 2v1 Dramatic Battle, and Rollback Netplay.

## Testing Decisions

- **Primary Seam — The Unified Frame-Stepped Scenario Session Seam:**
  - All gameplay, **core toggles**, **era styles**, **era gauges**, cross-era interactions, 2v1 Dramatic Battles, Training Mode telemetry, and Rollback snapshot round-trips will be tested through the existing top-level frame-stepped scenario session boundary (feeding a starting state + `MatchConfig` + frame-indexed player inputs, advancing frame by frame, and asserting external per-frame observables).
  - A good test asserts only externally observable outcomes—per-frame fighter positions, active moves, health, **era gauge** levels, hit/block outcomes, rendered `384×224` pixel buffers, sound-latch commands, training-overlay telemetry (hitbox rectangles and frame-meter states), and state-snapshot equality—never internal helper functions or private struct layouts.
- **Non-Regression of the Faithful Port Baseline:**
  - Every existing MAME 0.289 **comparison baseline** acceptance test (idle, movement, crouch, jump, normals, specials, Hadoken, blocking, CPU decisions, AI tables, round outcomes, audio chips, and the full fixed-fight milestone audit) must continue to pass with zero frame, pixel, RAM, or latch diffs when `MatchConfig` uses the `Faithful 1987` **ruleset preset**.
- **Differential Behavioral Tests for Core Toggles and Era Styles:**
  - Each **core toggle** is verified by running a paired scenario across the session seam with the toggle `false` vs. `true` under identical inputs (for example: proving a button press at frame $F$ starts an attack at $F+1$ with `positive_edge_attacks = true` vs. waiting for `release + 1` when `false`; proving a *Hadoken* released during a `0x91` walk frame fires a fireball with `interruptible_walk_cycle = true` vs. a standing punch when `false`; proving `sf-blocks-05` blocks a jumping heavy kick when crouching guard is fixed).
  - Cross-era and **era gauge** scenarios verify multi-hit combo connections against `SF1` defenders, throw teching and corner pushback, Parry/FADC/V-Trigger/Drive gauge consumption, and screen-freeze input retention.
- **Determinism & Snapshot Round-Trip Tests (Replay Takeover & Rollback Seam):**
  - Automated tests save a session snapshot at frame $N$, advance 60 frames to $N+60$, restore the snapshot at $N$, and re-simulate with identical inputs to $N+60$ (asserting bit-identical state, pixel hashes, and audio latches) and withbranched inputs (verifying **Replay Takeover** and rollback resimulation).
- **Auxiliary Seams (Asset Pack Validator & Host Smoke Check):**
  - An offline asset-pack validation test verifies that all generated overhaul sprite packs conform to the 15-color-plus-transparent `16×16` tile schema and that all new voice/SFX clips conform to the 4-bit 8 kHz MSM5205 ADPCM and YM2151 FM formats.
  - The existing narrow host smoke check in the development launcher verifies window creation, audio device playback, keyboard/controller bindings, and clean execution across both faithful and **overhaul** presets.

## Out of Scope

- Replacing the `384×224` 1987 pixel-art presentation with 3D models, high-resolution vector redraws, or unquantized truecolor sprites from later hardware generations.
- Adding playable guest characters or later-series fighters outside the twelve canonical participants of the 1987 tournament as required scope.
- Paid microtransactions, premium currency bundles, battle passes, or grind-locked characters, styles, or palette colors.
- Proprietary central matchmaking servers or geographic region locks (netplay is peer-to-peer rollback with direct room codes and an open lobby browser).
- Bundling or distributing copyrighted original arcade ROM files or commercial CD soundtrack rips in the repository.
