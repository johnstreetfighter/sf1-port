## Problem Statement

The faithful **port** is complete, and the overhaul tickets `O1`–`O21` and `M1`–`M8` are closed, yet a player cannot play any of it. The only way to start the game is the development launcher: subcommands and function keys, quick fights that begin from gitignored reference captures a player does not have, no title, no menu, no settings screen, no way to pick a fighter, and a `select` subcommand that draws the portrait grid but never starts a fight. The **core toggles**, the diagnostic overlay, replay takeover and the twelve-fighter binding do act on the real 1987 fight, but they are reached by editing a config file and pressing F-keys. Everything from the extended compositor onward was built as a separate combat model that never touches the 1987 machine, so there is nothing for a menu to reach there yet.

The player needs a game: launch it, see the 1987 cabinet come to life, press Start, choose a **play mode**, choose a fighter, and fight on the real 1987 engine under the **ruleset preset** of their choice, with settings that persist and a pad in hand. The developer needs that game to be a place later projects plug into (the era picker, World Tour, the online lobby) rather than plumbing rebuilt each time, and needs the faithful path to stay byte-identical throughout.

## Solution

Ship a player-facing game as its own binary with a 1987-styled **front end** drawn from the ROM's own tiles. The game opens on the faithful **attract** with every toggle off and `FREE PLAY` composited where the cabinet blinked `INSERT COIN`. Any Start opens a framed Main Menu: Single Player, Multi Player, Settings, 1987 and Exit, with the **preset line** at the foot of every framed screen. Single Player offers Arcade (Ryu or Ken on the 1987 **ladder**) and Training; Multi Player offers **Local** with all twelve **tournament fighters** on the portrait select; World Tour, Dramatic Battle and Online appear greyed until their projects land. Settings is five tabs across the top: Gameplay, Roster, Video, Sound and Controls, with a greyed Netplay tab.

Every fight is reached by the **cabinet driver** feeding coin and Start to the running faithful machine behind a black frame, then handing the stick to the player on the real country-select screen (ADR-0004). No fight state is ever written by hand and the engine gains no entry point and no behaviour change. The 1987 item opens a DIP screen and boots the **faithful cabinet** from power-on with coins and service mode intact; a held Esc is the only way back. Keyboard and DualShock 4 pads drive everything, rebinding happens in-game, and gameplay, bindings and settings persist in the app-data folder.

Under the hood the game is a separate binary, every screen is a pure state machine tested without a window, and the window, audio and input glue is a shared host crate the development launcher also uses (ADR-0005). A boss ladder report, generated by an ignored-by-default test, records how far each boss gets through the one-player game as the player, so the SF1+ project starts from a map.

## User Stories

### Launching and the attract

1. As a player, I want to double-click one executable and see the 1987 warning screen, title, demo fight and ranking play exactly as the cabinet showed them, so that the game opens the way the arcade did.
2. As a player, I want the attract to read `FREE PLAY` where the cabinet blinked `INSERT COIN`, so that I know Start alone is enough.
3. As a player, I want any Start press on the keyboard or either pad to open the Main Menu at any point of the attract, including the warning screen and the demo fight, so that I never wait for a title to come round.
4. As a player, I want the attract to pause behind the menu and resume where it was when I leave the menu, so that the cabinet feels continuously alive.
5. As a player, I want the game to open borderless fullscreen with the `ARCADE CABINET` look at true 4:3, with full volume, on first launch, so that the game is playable and looks right before I touch a setting. *(Amended for the video options spec, #203: this was "3× scale, windowed". `WINDOWED` still opens at 3×.)*
6. As a player without the ROM files in place, I want a framed screen naming the set the game expects, the three folders it looked in, and a `RETRY` and `EXIT`, so that I can drop the files in and continue without relaunching.

### The menus

7. As a player, I want a framed Main Menu with Single Player, Multi Player, Settings, 1987 and Exit, drawn in the ROM's font with the gold frame, the blue header and the yellow cursor of COUNTRY SELECT, so that the menu looks like part of the 1987 game.
8. As a player, I want the **preset line** at the foot of every framed screen to name the active **ruleset preset**, so that I always know which edition I am playing.
9. As a player, I want Single Player to offer Arcade Mode, Training, World Tour and Dramatic Battle, with the last two greyed until they exist, so that the menu tree is complete and honest.
10. As a player, I want Multi Player to offer Local, Online and Local co-op Dramatic, with the last two greyed until they exist, so that I know where online play will appear.
11. As a player, I want greyed items drawn in the dim blue of the preset line and skipped by the cursor, so that I cannot select what does not exist yet.
12. As a player, I want any player's stick, buttons or pad to move every menu and settings screen as one cursor, so that either of us can drive the menus.
13. As a player, I want Esc to mean back on every front-end screen and never to quit, and Exit to be the only item that closes the game, so that nothing closes by accident.
14. As a player, I want hard cuts between screens, as the ROM does, so that the front end never looks like a modern launcher.
15. As a player, I want a short ROM sound effect on cursor movement and another on confirm, and otherwise silent menus, so that the menus sound like the board.
16. As a player, I want the window's close button to quit at once from any screen or fight, so that closing means closing.

### Choosing a play mode and a fighter

## Implementation Decisions

- **Shape (ADR-0005).** The shipped game is a new binary holding only the window loop, settings loading, ROM lookup and the screen stack. Every screen (attract wrapper, main menu, single player, multi player, each settings tab, select, training setup, the 1987 cabinet's DIP screen, pause, after-match, ROM-missing) is a pure state machine in the engine crate that takes menu inputs and returns a frame and a transition, with no window types and no I/O. The development launcher's audio, bindings, window-open and present helpers move into a shared host crate together with the new pad reader; the launcher imports them and keeps every subcommand unchanged.
- **Reaching a fight (ADR-0004).** The cabinet driver turns a chosen play mode and fighters into the exact cabinet-input sequence that takes the running faithful machine from the attract to the country-select task: a coin pulse, then `start1` for one-player play modes or `start1` followed by the `start2` join for Local, stepped without rendering until the country-select task is reached or a frame budget runs out. The real country-select screen is then shown and P1 picks with the stick in every play mode. Before driving, the front end sets the machine's match configuration to the active preset with the roster settings from the select (play mode, P1, P2, full cast, four countries, ban list) and the difficulty and round-time DIP words from the Gameplay tab. The engine never reads a country from the configuration; that field is unused.
- **No engine changes.** Fighter binding, the four-country branch and the core toggles are read by the engine exactly as they are today. The Arcade stand-in rule is recorded in the design for the SF1+ project and not built.
- **The attract.** Runs the faithful machine with every toggle off and the factory DIP words. `FREE PLAY` is composited over the `INSERT COIN` position as a presentation overlay in overhaul play only. Any Start from the first frame after the power-on RAM clear opens the Main Menu; the machine is paused behind the menu. Before a drive the front end snapshots the attract machine.
- **Which machine each play mode keeps.** Arcade keeps the machine: the ROM's continue countdown (with the continuation DIP forced to allowed and a coin pulse injected when the continue screen opens, the ROM's continue limit still applying), game over, ranking check, name entry and ranking screen play as translated, and the front end shows the Main Menu when that flow would have returned to the attract; the ranking table lives in that machine for the session. Local and Training stop at the match end before the original's winner-carries-on branch, show the after-match box, and restore the attract snapshot when the player leaves. Rematch keeps fighters and country and replays the flag hidden.
- **Training** is the one-player game against the chosen CPU opponent, or a Local-type fight with an idle dummy on the P2 side, chosen on the Training screen. It adds nothing to the engine: the fight, the overlays, the pause box, snapshot and rewind, and the live preset row, which cycles among the overhaul presets and excludes Faithful 1987.
- **Preset changes** apply the preset's toggles and roster defaults as the configuration does today; the front end re-stamps the chosen P1 and P2 after every preset change and before every drive.
- **Faithful 1987 as the active preset** inside the front end: only Ryu and Ken selectable, Local is Ryu against Ken, the two-country screen, the preset line reads `FAITHFUL 1987`, nothing forced on.
- **Rendering.** Menus, the select, the pause box, the after-match box, the FREE PLAY text, the Esc hold bar and the DIP screen are drawn by the compositor from the ROM's 8×8 text font, the header tiles the translated COUNTRY SELECT draws, the gold frame and the existing portrait renderer, which gains a second (blue) cursor and greyed tiles with captions. Busts are decoded at startup from the ROM's VS-screen tiles. Fights render through the faithful hardware path plus the diagnostic overlay; the overlay module's era-gauge strip is not used. Hard cuts everywhere; black during the hidden drive; the fight dimmed under the pause box.
- **Sound.** Two of the ROM's verified FM effects for cursor movement and confirm, through the existing mixer; the attract's sound stops with its machine; master, FM and sample volumes applied in the host mixer.
- **Input.** Logical inputs stay as today (four directions, six buttons per player, cabinet keys) plus menu confirm, back, Start and a tab-switch pair. Defaults: Enter and Backspace, Q and E; LP confirms and LK goes back on the fight bindings; on a DualShock 4, Cross, Circle, Options, L1/R1. Pads through the pure-Rust pad crate with its default Windows Gaming Input backend (XInput opt-in unused), which reports the DualShock 4 as a raw game controller mapped through the SDL database; no rumble; events only while the window has focus; confirmed on the real pads by the host smoke check, with SDL2 as the fallback confined to the host crate. Pads map to P1 and P2 by the Controls tab or by connection order. A disconnect in Arcade, Local or Training opens the pause box with a notice and idles that side until a pad returns; online never pauses (recorded for the netplay project). Only the Classic control scheme is wired; Pneumatic and Modern are greyed.
- **Settings screens.** Five tabs across the top (Gameplay, Roster, Video, Sound, Controls) plus a greyed Netplay tab; `NAME ........ VALUE` rows with left and right; `ON`/`OFF`; frame counts as `4F`; difficulty 1 to 4 and round time by the DIP's values; a `RESET TAB` row ends every tab; saved on leaving the screen. Rebinding walks every logical input in order, a press binds, Esc keeps, duplicates within the same player are refused with a notice.
- **The faithful cabinet.** The 1987 item opens a DIP screen (coinage A, coinage B, continuation, buy-in, difficulty, round time, flip screen, demo sounds, attract music, country count, test mode; factory defaults; `POWER ON`; `HOLD ESC ONE SECOND TO RETURN` where the preset line sits). Power on boots a fresh faithful machine with those DIP words, cabinet keys live, nothing from the overhaul settings except video, sound and bindings. A one-second Esc hold, shown as a small hold-bar overlay, is the only exit.
- **Persistence.** Gameplay and roster in the existing match-config file; bindings in the existing bindings file, extended with pad bindings and menu inputs; video, sound, ROM folder, DIP words and last menu positions in a new settings file; all under the app-data folder; a file that fails to parse is logged and replaced by defaults.
- **ROM lookup and shipping.** One executable with the ROM manifest embedded; lookup beside the executable, then the app-data folder, then the settings file's folder; a ROM-missing screen in the compositor's ROM-independent fallback font with the set name, the three folders and `RETRY` and `EXIT`.
- **Log.** One file in the app-data folder, overwritten per launch: settings in force, ROM folder and manifest result, pads seen or lost, settings files that failed to parse, untranslated-branch notices with frame and addresses.
- **Defaults on first launch.** Borderless fullscreen with `ARCADE CABINET` at `TRUE 4:3` (amended by #203; `WINDOWED` opens at 3×), vsync on, volumes 10, Definitive Overhaul, keyboard for both players until a pad is seen. The window title is `Street Fighter`; the preset line names the edition.
- **Ladder report.** An ignored-by-default integration test drives each of the ten bosses through the one-player game as the player under Definitive Overhaul, records the first untranslated branch per stage and whether each mirror match runs, and writes a committed report under the reference docs. It never fails the build.

## Testing Decisions

- **What makes a good test.** It drives the product from the outside with inputs and asserts only what a player or a comparison could observe: the presented 384×224 pixels at known tile positions, the current screen's name and transition, the machine's task position and game-mode byte, the bound move table on the human side, the sound latch, snapshot equality, and that no untranslated branch was reached. It never asserts private screen state, helper functions or struct layouts.
- **Primary seam (new): the headless game session.** The game without its window, audio device and pads: per-frame logical inputs for both players, menu and cabinet, from power-on, yielding the presented frame, the sound latch, the current screen and its transition. Acceptance tests walk the product here: power-on, attract, `FREE PLAY` pixels, Start at several attract phases, every menu item and greyed item, each play mode into its fight (Ryu and Ken in Arcade; each of the twelve in Local and Training; Faithful 1987 reducing the select to Ryu and Ken and the two-country screen), the country pick, the pause box and each row, Training's live preset and restart round, the after-match box and rematch, Arcade's free continue through the ROM's game-over flow and back to the menu, Local's stop before the winner-carries-on branch and the attract resuming from its snapshot, the settings tabs with every row changed and read back, rebinding with a refused duplicate, the DIP screen into the faithful cabinet and the Esc hold back, and the ROM-missing screen's retry.
- **Existing seam: the frame-stepped scenario session.** Every existing comparison fixture runs unchanged with the toggles off. The attract wrapper is checked against the power-on scenario for a full attract loop. The cabinet driver is verified on the real power-on machine for each play mode and fighter, asserting the country-select task, the pre-fight, the bound move table and a clean first round. The ladder report test lives here.
- **Existing seam: the host smoke check**, extended to the new binary: window creation, audio device, keyboard, pad enumeration (the DualShock 4 in particular), settings files round trip in a scratch app-data folder, and ROM lookup in each of the three folders.
- **Determinism.** Pausing and resuming at frame N equals not pausing; restart round restores the round-start snapshot bit for bit, using the snapshot comparison the replay tests already do.
- **Prior art.** The character-select screen's input-driven tests with pixel assertions on the compositor's frame; the power-on boot, full one-player and full two-player scenario tests; the replay takeover snapshot round-trip tests; the development launcher's host smoke check and bindings round-trip test.

## Out of Scope

- New sprite or audio art of any kind; the bosses keep their 1987 move sets.
- Any engine change: the stand-in rule, mirror-match fixes, frozen health or timer, control-scheme hold-strength or motion synthesis, and whatever the ladder report finds all belong to later projects.
- Arcade with the ten bosses as the player; era styles, era gauges, SF1+ move sets, the twelve endings, World Tour, Dramatic Battle, online play, the jukebox and the asset pipeline. Their items appear greyed.
- The Pneumatic and Modern control schemes (greyed), a CRT or scanline filter, mouse support, Bluetooth pad testing, rumble, persisting the ranking table across launches, and any change to the development launcher's subcommands or fixtures.
